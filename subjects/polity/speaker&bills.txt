## Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93)

* Each House of Parliament shall have its own presiding officer.
* In Lok Sabha, we have a Speaker and a few Deputy Speakers.

### Background of the Institution of Speaker

1. **1921:** In this year, the Institution of Speaker & Deputy Speaker were first originated in India via the Government of India Act 1919.
2. After Partition, the Indian side followed the same system.
3. **G. V. Mavalankar** was the first Speaker of Lok Sabha after independence.

### Election & Tenure of Speaker

1. The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha from amongst its Members.
2. The date of election of the Speaker is fixed by the President.
3. Usually, the Speaker remains in office during the life of Lok Sabha. He has to vacate the office in 3 cases:
* **a)** He is not a member of Lok Sabha.
* **b)** Resigns in writing to the Deputy Speaker.
* **c)** He is removed by a Resolution.



### Removal of the Speaker

1. The resolution for removal must be passed by an **Absolute Majority**.
2. **14 days advance notice** is mandatory to move the resolution.
3. Whenever the Lok Sabha is dissolved, the Speaker does not vacate his office and continues till the newly elected Lok Sabha meets.

---

## Power & Functions of Speaker

1. Maintenance of order & decorum of the House.
2. Final interpretation of provisions of:
* **a)** Constitution of India
* **b)** Rules of Procedure of Parliament
* **c)** Parliamentary Precedents / Conventions


3. He **cannot vote in the first instance** on any resolution or bill, but he exercises a **casting vote** in case of a tie.
4. He adjourns the House / suspends the meeting in absence of a **Quorum** ($1/10^{\text{th}}$ of the total strength of the House $\rightarrow$ 55 members as per present strength in Lok Sabha).
5. He presides over the **Joint Sitting** of Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha (**Article 108**).
6. He decides whether a bill is a **Money Bill** or not, and his decision on this question is final (*Biggest Power*).
7. Speaker can **disqualify a member** of the House (*Biggest Power*).

### Mains Focus: Impartiality of Lok Sabha Speaker?

* Resignation from political party.
* **British Model:** *"Once a speaker, always a speaker."*
* $\rightarrow$ Need change in the Indian System.

## Deputy Speaker (Article 93)

1. He is also elected like the Speaker by Lok Sabha from amongst its Members.
2. He is elected **after** the election of the Speaker.
3. The date of election of the Deputy Speaker will be **fixed by the Speaker** (*Not by the President*).
4. Deputy Speaker is **NOT subordinate** to the Speaker (Not अधीन).
5. He resigns in writing to the Speaker of Lok Sabha.

### Submission of Resignation Summary

* **Speaker** $\rightarrow$ Deputy Speaker
* **Deputy Speaker** $\rightarrow$ Speaker
* **MPs** $\rightarrow$ Speaker
* **Minister** $\rightarrow$ President

## Pro-Tem Speaker (Article 95)

* Is the **oldest person** of Lok Sabha.
* The Speaker Pro-Tem facilitates the transition of the institution (Lok Sabha) from old members to newly elected members.
* The primary job of Speaker Pro-Tem is to enable the House to elect a new Speaker and administer oath to the newly elected MPs.
* **President Himself** administers oath to the Speaker Pro-Tem.
* His office is a temporary office, but the Pro-Tem Speaker possesses all the powers of a Speaker.

---

## Panel of Chairpersons of Lok Sabha

1. Under the rules of Lok Sabha, the Speaker nominates from amongst the members a panel of **not more than 10 Chairpersons**.
* **Absent** $\rightarrow$ Temporary
* **Vacant** $\rightarrow$ Permanent (Seat खाली होना / vacant seat)


2. Any of them can preside over the House in the **absence** (*not in case of vacancy*) of the Speaker or Deputy Speaker.

---

## Chairperson of Rajya Sabha (Article 89)

* Works same as Speaker of Lok Sabha.
* The **Vice President of India** is the *ex-officio* Chairperson of Rajya Sabha.
* As presiding officer, the powers & functions of Chairperson in Rajya Sabha are similar to those of the Speaker of Lok Sabha, **BUT** he is **not a member** of the House (unlike the Speaker).
* He also **cannot vote in the first instance** like the Speaker and only has a **casting vote**.
* While a Speaker can vote in the first instance when a resolution for his removal is under consideration of Lok Sabha, the Chairperson **CANNOT vote** because he is not a member of Rajya Sabha.

### Special Powers of the Speaker (Not available to Chairperson of Rajya Sabha):

1. The Speaker decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not.
2. The Speaker presides over a Joint Sitting of the two Houses.
$\rightarrow$ Both of these powers are **NOT available** to the Chairperson of Rajya Sabha.

---

## Secretariat of Parliament (Article 98)

* Each House of the Parliament has a separate secretariat staff of its own.
* Their recruitment & service conditions are regulated by the Parliament.
* The Secretariat of each House is headed by a **Secretary-General** (Permanent officer of the House) and is appointed by the Presiding Officer.

## Leader of Opposition (LOP)

### Salary & Allowances Act of LOP, 1977

* This Act defines LOP.
* LOP is the Leader of the **Largest Opposition Party**.
* **Largest Party Definition:** Party having a **minimum 10% seats** of Lok Sabha.
* Sushma Swaraj – LOP till 2014
* 2014–2024 – NO LOP in India
* 2024 – Rahul Gandhi


* Also known as *"Alternative PM"*.
* LOP is **not mentioned in the Constitution**, but it is mentioned in a law (Statutory recognition).

## Classification & Types of Bills

### According to Origin & Introduction:

* **Government Bill:** Proposed by a Minister.
* **Private Member Bill:** Proposed by any other MP or MLA.

### Types of Bills (by function):

* **Repeal Bills (खारिज करना):** Bill used to repeal existing law (e.g., Farm Laws).
* **Merger Bills:** Multiple laws are combined to make a new law (e.g., Labour Codes).
* **Amending Laws:** Amendment in the Constitution.
* **Renewal Laws:** Reservation extension (e.g., SC/ST reservation in election - 104th AA).

| Feature / Type | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill (Article 110) | Financial Bill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Goal** | To implement something new | Allocation of money to something already existing (e.g., Tax, Fund, Penalty) | Allocation of money to something new (e.g., GST) |
| **House Powers** | Lok Sabha = Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha has **more powers** than Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha = Rajya Sabha |
| **Presidential Action** | Yes / No / Return | Yes / Hold (**Cannot Return**) | Yes / No / Return |

## Money Bill (Article 110)

* **Definition:** Allocation of money to something that already exists.
* Can be introduced by a **Minister ONLY** (Never by a Private Member).

### Process Flow:

1. **Speaker Certification:** If certified ("Yes").
2. **Presidential Recommendation:** Goes directly to the President of India for recommendation ("Yes").
3. **Introduction:** Formally introduced by a Minister **ONLY in Lok Sabha** (*Never in Rajya Sabha*).
4. **Discussion & Passage:**
* First discussion takes place in Lok Sabha (Passed with **Simple Majority**).
* Moves to Rajya Sabha.
* Rajya Sabha can: **Pass / Reject / Amend** or **Hold Bill for 14 days**.
* *(Note: Rejecting has no value; changes suggested by Rajya Sabha can or cannot be accepted by Lok Sabha).*
* **Joint Sitting is NOT APPLICABLE to a Money Bill.**


5. **President's Action:**
* $\rightarrow$ **Yes**
* $\rightarrow$ **No**
* $\rightarrow$ **Hold**
* $\rightarrow$ **Return ✖** (*Cannot return Money Bill for reconsideration*)



### Ingredients of Money Bill (Article 110):

1. **Taxes:** Any existing tax addition, deduction, omission (हटाना), or alteration.
2. **Funds:**
* **Consolidated Fund of India** (मजबूत / Main Fund) - Money In/Out
* **Contingency Fund of India** (Emergency fund) - Kept with the President
* *(Note: The biggest fund of the Government is the Consolidated Fund of India).*


3. **Parliamentary Sanction:** Done under Money Bill.
4. **Borrowing:** Governed under Money Bill.

> **Exceptions:** Fees, licenses, registration charges, challans, or local body taxes (PRIs / MCs) do **NOT** come under Money Bill.

## Journey of a Bill (Ordinary Bill to Act)

```
[ Stage I: Pre-Parliamentary Stage ]
             │
             ▼
[ Stage II: Parliamentary Stage ]
  ├── First Reading (Introduction)
  ├── Second Reading (Clause-by-Clause Discussion & Amendments)
  └── Third Reading (Voting: Yes / No)
             │
             ▼
[ Stage III: Post-Parliamentary Stage ]
  └── Presidential Assent (Yes / No / Return)

```

### Stage Details:

* **Stage I: Pre-Parliamentary Stage (Drafting)**
* Drafting of Bills by Committee & Secretary / Academicians / Retired Judges / Lawyers.
* $1^{\text{st}}$ draft of law is prepared, and the Law Ministry creates its revised draft and sends it to the concerned Ministry.


* **Stage II: Parliamentary Stage**
* **First Reading:** Introduction of the Bill; opened for introduction.
* **Second Reading:** Discussions are done clause-by-clause. Amendments can be made during this stage.
* **Third Reading:** NO DISCUSSION; direct voting ($\text{Yes} / \text{No}$) for final decision.


* **Stage III: Post-Parliamentary Stage**
* Bill goes to President / Governor, who can say:
* $\rightarrow$ **Yes**
* $\rightarrow$ **No**
* $\rightarrow$ **Return the Bill**





*(Note: Financial Bills follow the exact same process as Ordinary Bills).*
