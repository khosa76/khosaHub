PARLIAMENT OF INDIA



1. Introduction & Composition of Parliament

Constitutional Framework (Part V, Articles 79–122):

  • Article 79: Parliament consists of President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha. (The President is an integral part of Parliament but not a member of either House).
  • State Legislature Equivalent: Governor + Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad.
  • Bicameral States (Vidhan Parishad present): Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, Telangana, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh (6 States).

I. Houses of Parliament Compared

  1. Lok Sabha (House of the People / Lower House):
    • Strength & Election: 543 members elected directly by the people via First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system.
    • Tenure: Maximum 5 years (unless dissolved earlier by President).
    • Council of Ministers Limit: Capped at 15% of Lok Sabha strength (91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003).
  2. Rajya Sabha (Council of States / Upper House):
    • Strength: 245 members (229 States, 4 UTs, 12 Nominated by President for art, literature, science, social service).
    • Election System: Elected indirectly by MLAs using Proportional Representation by Means of Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV).
    • Tenure: Permanent House (not subject to dissolution); 1/3rd of members retire every 2 years (individual MP term is 6 years).

II. Daily Proceedings & Annual Sessions

Daily Sitting Schedule:

  • 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM: Question Hour (Starred/Unstarred questions asked to Ministers).
  • 12:00 PM – 1:00 PM: Zero Hour (Indian innovation, non-statutory, for urgent issues).
  • 2:00 PM – 6:00 PM: Legislative Business & Discussion on Bills.

3 Annual Sessions: Budget Session (Feb–May), Monsoon Session (July–Sept), Winter Session (Nov–Dec). Maximum gap between sessions cannot exceed 6 months.

Key Terms:

  • Adjournment: Terminates a daily sitting (done by Presiding Officer).
  • Prorogation: Terminates a session of the House (done by President).
  • Dissolution: Ends the life of the Lok Sabha (done by President).


2. Speaker & Presiding Officers

I. Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93)

  • Historical Origin: Created in India in 1921 under the Government of India Act, 1919. G.V. Mavlankar was the first Speaker post-independence.
  • Election: Elected by Lok Sabha members from amongst themselves. Date of election is fixed by the President.
  • Tenure & Removal: Remains in office during the life of Lok Sabha. Removed only by a resolution passed by an absolute majority of all the then members of Lok Sabha after a 14-day advance notice.
  • Dissolution Rule: Does NOT vacate office when Lok Sabha dissolves; continues until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha.

Constitutional Powers of Speaker:

  1. Decorum & Interpretation: Maintains order; final interpreter of Constitution, Rules of Procedure, and Precedents inside Lok Sabha.
  2. Casting Vote: Does not vote in the first instance, but exercises a Casting Vote in case of a tie.
  3. Quorum: Adjourns or suspends House if Quorum (1/10th of total strength) is absent.
  4. Joint Sittings (Article 108): Presides over Joint Sittings of both Houses.
  5. Money Bills (Article 110): Decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not; decision is FINAL and unchallengeable.
  6. Anti-Defection Disqualification: Decides disqualification of MPs under the 10th Schedule.

II. Deputy Speaker, Pro-Tem Speaker & Rajya Sabha Chair

  • Deputy Speaker (Article 93): Elected by Lok Sabha after Speaker election (date fixed by Speaker). NOT subordinate to Speaker; directly responsible to Lok Sabha. Resigns in writing to the Speaker.
  • Pro-Tem Speaker (Article 95): Appointed by President (usually the senior-most member) to administer oath to newly elected MPs and conduct Speaker election. Administered oath by the President.
  • Panel of Chairpersons: Speaker nominates up to 10 members to preside in the absence of Speaker/Deputy Speaker.
  • Chairperson of Rajya Sabha (Article 89): The Vice-President of India is ex-officio Chairperson. Cannot vote in first instance; cannot vote during removal proceedings because he is not an MP.


3. Secretariat & Leader of Opposition

Parliamentary Secretariat (Article 98):

Each House of Parliament has a separate secretarial staff headed by a Secretary-General (rank of Cabinet Secretary), appointed by the Presiding Officer.

Leader of Opposition (LoP):

  • Qualification: Leader of the largest opposition party having at least 10% of total seats (55 seats in LS).
  • Statutory Status: Accorded statutory recognition under the Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977.
  • Role: Entitled to cabinet minister salary and perks; often referred to as the "Alternative Prime Minister".

II. Special Procedure for Money Bills (Article 110)

  1. Introduced ONLY in Lok Sabha with prior recommendation of the President.
  2. Requires Speaker's endorsed certificate stating it is a Money Bill.
  3. Rajya Sabha has strictly 14 days to return it with suggestions. Lok Sabha can accept or reject all suggestions.
  4. If Rajya Sabha does not return it in 14 days, it is deemed passed by both Houses.
  5. President can give assent or withhold assent, but CANNOT return it for reconsideration.

III. 3-Stage Journey of an Ordinary Bill

  • Stage 1 (Pre-Parliamentary & 1st Reading): Drafting by Ministry; Introduction & publication in Gazette.
  • Stage 2 (2nd & 3rd Reading): Detailed clause-by-clause scrutiny, committee reference, voting on amendments, final voting.
  • Stage 3 (Presidential Assent): Article 111 — President gives assent, withholds assent, or returns bill for reconsideration.


5. Parliamentary Motions & Privileges

I. Important Parliamentary Motions

  • No-Confidence Motion (Article 75): Table only in Lok Sabha against Council of Ministers. Requires support of 50 members. If passed, Cabinet must resign. No specific reasons required.
  • Censure Motion: Can be moved against individual minister or entire council for specific policies. Reasons must be stated.
  • Adjournment Motion: To draw attention to a matter of urgent public importance. Requires support of 50 members; interrupts normal business for 2.5+ hours.
  • Motion of Thanks: Moved on President's address. Must be passed by both Houses; failure means government defeat.

II. Annual Financial Statement / Budget (Article 112)

  • Revenue Budget: Short-term operational expenses & receipts (Subsidies, Pensions / Tax & Non-tax Revenue).
  • Capital Budget: Long-term asset creation & liability acquisition (Defence Infra, Loans / Borrowings, Disinvestment).

III. Parliamentary Privileges (Articles 105 & 194)

Article 105 (MPs) & Article 194 (MLAs):

  • Individual Privileges: Absolute freedom of speech on the floor of the House; cannot be prosecuted in any court for anything said or voted in Parliament.
  • Freedom from Civil Arrest: Cannot be arrested in civil cases during session, or 40 days before & 40 days after session.
  • Crucial Exception: Privileges do NOT extend to criminal offenses or destruction of public property!
