PARLIAMENT OF INDIA (FULL-FLEDGED UPSC MASTER NOTES)



1. Organization, Composition, Delimitation & Dissolution

Constitutional Framework (Part V, Articles 79–122):

  • Article 79: Parliament consists of President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha. The President is an integral part of Parliament (President-in-Parliament, Westminster model) but not a member of either House.
  • Article 80 & 81: Composition of Rajya Sabha (Art 80: max 250 members; 238 elected by State/UT MLAs via STV + 12 nominated by President from Literature, Science, Art, Social Service) and Lok Sabha (Art 81: max 550 members; directly elected from territorial constituencies).
  • Article 85: President summons, prorogues Parliament sessions, and dissolves Lok Sabha. Max gap between two sessions cannot exceed 6 months.
  • Article 94 & 99: Speaker vacation of office (Art 94); Oath or affirmation by MPs before President or nominee (Art 99).
  • Article 103 & 109: President decides disqualification of MPs on Election Commission opinion (Art 103); Special procedure for Money Bills (Art 109).
  • Article 113 & 118: Demands for Grants & Estimates procedure (Art 113); Rules of Procedure of Parliament (Art 118).
  • Comparison with USA: US Congress consists of Senate + House of Representatives. The US President follows strict separation of powers and is NOT part of Congress.
  • Bicameral States (6 States): Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka.
  • 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019: Discontinued nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian members to Lok Sabha and State Assemblies (ceased Jan 25, 2020). Extended SC/ST seat reservations up to 2030.
  • History & Hindi Nomenclature: Parliament was designed by chief architect Sir Edwin Lutyens on Raisina Hill, New Delhi. In 1954, the Hindi names "Rajya Sabha" and "Lok Sabha" were officially adopted in place of "Council of States" and "House of the People".
  • Rules of Procedure vs. Constitution: The Constitution provides the foundational concepts (e.g. Speaker, Money Bill, Quorum). Detailed day-to-day mechanics are governed by the "Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business" under Art 118.

Core Mathematical Equations of Legislative Structure:

  • Parliament Equation: Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha
  • State Legislature Equation: State Legislature = Governor + Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad (in 6 Bicameral States)
  • Governor's Role: The Governor is appointed by the President of India and forms a part of the State Legislature, but is NOT a member of either House.

I. Houses of Parliament Compared

  1. Lok Sabha (House of the People / Lower House):
    • Strength: Max 550 (Currently 543 members: 524 States, 19 UTs via UTs Direct Election Act 1965).
    • Electoral System: Territorial Representation using First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system based on Universal Adult Franchise (Voting age reduced from 21 to 18 by 61st CAA 1988).
    • Tenure: 5 years from first meeting. Extended by 1 year at a time during National Emergency (Art 352), max 6 months post-emergency.
  2. Rajya Sabha (Council of States / Upper House):
    • Strength & 4th Schedule: Max 250 (Currently 245 members: 225 States, 8 UTs [Delhi-3, Puducherry-1, J&K-4], 12 Nominated by President for Art, Literature, Science, Social Service).
    • Electoral System: Indirectly elected by elected MLAs using Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV). Seats allocated based on state population (unlike US Senate's equal 2 seats per state).
    • Tenure: Permanent continuing chamber. 1/3rd members retire every 2nd year. MP term is 6 years (fixed by Representation of the People Act 1951, NOT Constitution).

Assembly & Lok Sabha Constituency Extremes:

  • State Legislative Assemblies (Vidhan Sabha Size):
    • Largest Assembly: Uttar Pradesh (403 seats).
    • Smallest Assembly: Sikkim (32 seats).
  • Lok Sabha Constituencies (Geographical Area):
    • Largest Constituency by Area: Ladakh (vast geographic area, low population density).
    • Smallest Constituency by Area: Chandni Chowk, Delhi (dense urban footprint, smallest area).

II. System of Delimitation & Constituency Readjustment

  • Uniformity Principles: Ratio between seats assigned and population of a State must be uniform across all States (exempt for States with population < 6 million). Population-to-seat ratio must be uniform across all constituencies within a State.
  • Key Delimitation Amendments:
    • 42nd CAA 1976: Froze seat allocation and constituency division based on 1971 Census till 2000.
    • 84th CAA 2001: Extended seat freeze till 2026. Allowed readjustment of territorial constituencies using 1991 Census. Refixed SC/ST reserved seats.
    • 87th CAA 2003: Authorized delimitation based on 2001 Census without altering total state seat counts.

Detailed Analysis of FPTP (Plurality) System:

  • Merits of FPTP:
    1. Simplicity: Easy to understand for ordinary voters without complex math.
    2. Stability: Tends to produce clear parliamentary majorities for stable governance.
    3. Direct Choice: Voters directly select local representative candidates.
    4. Broad-based Participation: Encourages parties to appeal to broad segments of society.
  • Demerits of FPTP:
    1. Exclusion of Small Parties: Smaller or minority political parties struggle to gain representation.
    2. Identity Politics: Can encourage candidates to rely on local caste, religion, or ethnic vote banks.
    3. Seat-Vote Outcome Distortion: A political party securing a majority of total votes nationwide may fail to win a majority of seats in the House.

VI. Sessions Terminology & Termination Matrix

  • Session of Parliament: Period between the first sitting of the House and its prorogation (or dissolution).
  • Recess: The period between the prorogation of Parliament and its reassembly in a new session.
  • Adjournment Sine Die: Terminating a sitting of Parliament without assigning a specific day or time for reassembling (done by Presiding Officer).

VII. Lame-Duck Session

Definition: Refers to the LAST session of the outgoing Lok Sabha after elections for the new Lok Sabha have been held.

Lame-Duck MPs: Members of the outgoing Lok Sabha who fail to get re-elected to the new House.

Historical Context: Held in 1957 and 1962 to pass a Vote on Account before the new House met. Not held in 1967 as the 4th Lok Sabha was constituted immediately.



2. Qualifications, Disqualifications, Seat Vacancy & Oath

Legal & Constitutional Classifications (Key Concepts):

  • Un-Constitutional: Declared null and void under Articles 13, 32, or 226 because it falls outside the scope of or directly violates the Constitution.
  • Non-Constitutional (Statutory): Not written in the original text of the Constitution, but created legally through an Act of Parliament (e.g. Reserve Bank of India - RBI, Life Insurance Corporation - LIC, NITI A).
  • Extra-Constitutional: Concepts or administrative bodies derived or expanded outside the constitutional text (e.g. National Commission for Women - NCW).

I. Qualifications & Constitutional Disqualifications

  • Constitutional Qualifications (Article 84): Citizen of India; Age ≥ 25 years for Lok Sabha, ≥ 30 years for Rajya Sabha; Oath before ECI authorized person. Candidate for Rajya Sabha needs to be an elector anywhere in India (2003 RPA amendment removed state domicile requirement, upheld by SC in 2006).
  • Disqualifications under Constitution (Article 102(1)):
    • Holds any Office of Profit under Union/State Govt (except Minister or statutory exemptions).
    • Unsound mind (declared by court), undischarged insolvent, non-citizen/voluntary foreign allegiance.
    • Decision Authority: President's decision is final, but President MUST act according to the binding opinion of the Election Commission of India (ECI).
  • Disqualifications under Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1951:
    • Guilty of corrupt electoral practices; Convicted with imprisonment for ≥ 2 years (disqualified during imprisonment + 6 years post-release; preventive detention does NOT disqualify).
    • Failed to lodge election expense accounts; Dismissed for corruption/disloyalty; Interest in govt contracts/corporations (≥25% govt share).
    • Challenge Authority: High Court original jurisdiction on election petitions; appeal lies with Supreme Court.

II. Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule)

Grounds for Disqualification under 10th Schedule (52nd CAA 1985 & 91st CAA 2003):

  1. Party Member: Voluntarily gives up party membership OR votes/abstains contrary to party whip without prior permission (not condoned within 15 days).
  2. Independent Member: Joins ANY political party after election.
  3. Nominated Member: Joins any political party AFTER expiry of 6 months from taking seat.

Exemption: Merger of political party if at least 2/3rd of members agree to the merger (91st CAA 2003 abolished the 1/3rd split rule).

Deciding Authority: Presiding Officer (Speaker/Chairman). Judicial review applies (Kihoto Hollohan Case 1992 — Speaker acts as a tribunal).

III. Vacation of Seats & Double Membership (RPA 1951 Rules)

  1. Elected to Both Houses of Parliament: Must intimate within 10 days which House he wishes to serve. Default: Rajya Sabha seat becomes vacant.
  2. Sitting Member of One House Elected to Other: Seat in the first House automatically becomes vacant.
  3. Elected to Two Seats in Same House: Must exercise option for one seat; default: BOTH seats become vacant.
  4. Parliament & State Legislature: Cannot hold both. Must resign state legislature seat within 14 days; default: Parliament seat becomes vacant (Prohibition of Simultaneous Membership Rules 1950).
  5. Absence without Permission: House can declare seat vacant if MP is absent for 60 consecutive days (excluding prorogation/adjournment > 4 consecutive days).

IV. Oath & Statutory Penalty

Every MP takes an oath before the President or an authorized representative. Under Article 104, a penalty of ₹500 per day is imposed if a person sits or votes in Parliament before taking oath, or knowing they are disqualified/prohibited.



3. Presiding Officers, Party Officers & Parliamentary Rights

I. Speaker & Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha (Article 93)

  • Historical Background: The institutions of Speaker and Deputy Speaker originated in India in 1921 under the provisions of the Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms). Following independence, G. V. Mavalankar became the first Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • Speaker Election & Tenure: Elected by Lok Sabha members from amongst themselves. Date fixed by the President. Holds office during the life of Lok Sabha. Ranks 7th in Order of Precedence (equal to CJI, above Cabinet Ministers).
  • Vacation of Office: Vacates office if he ceases to be a Lok Sabha member, resigns in writing to the Deputy Speaker, or is removed by resolution.
  • Removal: Resolution passed by an Absolute / Effective Majority (majority of all the THEN members of the House) after mandatory 14 days advance notice supported by ≥ 50 members. Speaker cannot vote in 1st instance during removal proceedings, but can vote in case of tie. Does not vacate office upon Lok Sabha dissolution; continues till newly elected Lok Sabha meets.
  • Powers & Quorum: Maintains order & decorum. Final interpreter of Constitution, Rules of Procedure, and Parliamentary Precedents. Cannot vote in 1st instance, but exercises a casting vote in case of a tie. Adjourns/suspends House in absence of a Quorum (1/10th of total strength = 55 members). Disqualifies members under 10th Schedule (Anti-Defection).
  • British Model vs. India: In the UK, the Speaker resigns from his political party upon election (*"Once a speaker, always a speaker"*). In India, the Speaker retains party membership, though expected to act with total impartiality.
  • Deputy Speaker: Elected by Lok Sabha after the Speaker's election; date fixed by the Speaker. Conventionally belongs to the Opposition. NOT subordinate to the Speaker; directly responsible to Lok Sabha. Resigns in writing to the Speaker.
  • Pro-Tem Speaker (Article 95): Senior-most member of Lok Sabha appointed by the President (who administers oath to him). Facilitates the transition of the House, administers oath to newly elected MPs, and enables the election of a new Speaker. Temporary office, but possesses full powers of a Speaker.
  • Panel of Chairpersons of Lok Sabha: Speaker nominates a panel of not more than 10 Chairpersons. Any of them can preside over the House in the ABSENCE of the Speaker or Deputy Speaker. Critical Distinction: A panel member CANNOT preside during a VACANCY (permanent empty seat); in case of vacancy, the President appoints a member until an election is held.

📋 Resignation Submission Summary Matrix:

  • Speaker of Lok Sabha → Resigns to the Deputy Speaker
  • Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha → Resigns to the Speaker
  • Member of Parliament (MP) → Resigns to the Presiding Officer (Speaker / Chairman)
  • Union Minister → Resigns to the President of India

Exclusive Powers of Lok Sabha Speaker:

  1. Final certifying authority for Money Bills (Art 110) — Unchallengeable in court or Parliament.
  2. Presides over Joint Sitting of Parliament (Art 108) (Power NOT available to RS Chairman).
  3. Ex-officio Chairman of Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG), Conference of Presiding Officers, and heads Business Advisory, Rules, and General Purposes Committees.

II. Chairman & Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Article 89)

  • Chairman: Vice-President of India is ex-officio Chairman. Not a member of Rajya Sabha. Exercises casting vote only in case of a tie. Cannot vote in 1st instance even during his removal proceedings. Cannot decide Money Bills or preside over Joint Sittings. Removed only via removal as Vice-President.
  • Deputy Chairman: Elected by Rajya Sabha from amongst its members. Resigns in writing to the Chairman. Directly responsible to Rajya Sabha.

III. Secretariat of Parliament (Article 98)

  • Article 98 Mandate: Each House of Parliament has a separate Secretariat staff of its own, whose recruitment and service conditions are regulated by Parliamentary law.
  • Secretary-General: The Secretariat of each House is headed by a Secretary-General—a permanent, high-ranking officer of the House appointed directly by the Presiding Officer (Speaker/Chairman).

IV. Leaders & Office of the Whip

Whip: Based on Parliamentary Conventions (Not in Constitution, Rules of House, or Statute). Appointed by political parties as assistant floor manager to enforce discipline. Defiance leads to 10th Schedule disqualification.

Leader of Opposition (LoP): Given statutory recognition under the Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977. Recognized party must hold at least 10% total strength (55 seats in LS). Cabinet rank status; referred to as "Alternative PM".
Historical Timeline: Sushma Swaraj was LoP till 2014 → 2014–2024: NO recognized LoP in India (no opposition party secured 10% seats) → 2024: Rahul Gandhi appointed LoP.

IV. Rights of Ministers & Attorney General (Article 88)

  • Every Union Minister and the Attorney General of India has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of EITHER House, Joint Sittings, and any Parliamentary Committee to which they are named.
  • CRITICAL RESTRICTION: They CANNOT vote unless they are a member of that specific House.
  • A non-MP minister can participate for up to 6 months without holding a seat in either House.

V. Parliamentary Privileges (Article 105 & Article 194)

Definition & Scope: Special rights, immunities, and exemptions enjoyed by Houses of Parliament, their committees, and their members (Article 105 for MPs; Article 194 for MLAs/MLCs in States).

  • Individual Privileges:
    1. Freedom from Arrest: Cannot be arrested in civil cases during a session and 40 days before/after session. (Does NOT apply to criminal cases or preventive detention).
    2. Freedom of Speech in Parliament: No MP/MLA is liable in any court for anything said or vote given inside the House.
    3. Exemption from Jury/Witness Service: Exempt from appearing as witness in court when Parliament is in session.
  • Collective Privileges of the House:
    1. Right to publish debates/reports and prohibit others from publishing them.
    2. Right to exclude strangers and hold secret sessions.
    3. Right to punish members/outsiders for breach of privilege or contempt of the House (Commitment).
    4. Courts are barred from inquiring into parliamentary proceedings (Article 122).


4. Daily Proceedings, Quorum, Voting & Language

I. Daily Sitting Schedule & Quorum

Quorum (Article 100): Minimum 1/10th of total membership of the House including Presiding Officer (Lok Sabha: 55; Rajya Sabha: 25). In absence of quorum, Presiding Officer MUST adjourn or suspend the meeting.

Daily Schedule:

  • 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM (Question Hour): Starred (oral/supplementaries allowed - Green), Unstarred (written/no supplementaries - White), Short Notice (< 10 days notice - Light Pink), Questions to Private Members (Yellow).
  • 12:00 PM – 1:00 PM (Zero Hour): Informal Indian innovation (since 1962), non-statutory, raised without prior notice immediately after Question Hour.
  • 2:00 PM – 6:00 PM (Legislative Business): Discussion and voting on Bills, Motions, and Budget.

II. Methods of Voting in Lok Sabha

  1. Voice Vote: Simplest method ("Ayes" vs "Noes"). Presiding Officer judges orally.
  2. Division Vote: Triggered if Voice Vote is challenged. Lobbies cleared (3 min 30 sec). Done via Automatic Vote Recording Equipment (Digital: Green=Aye, Red=No, Yellow=Abstain), Ayes/Noes Slips, or physical count.
  3. Secret Ballot: Used for Speaker/Presidential elections (White LED flashes on vote).

III. Language in Parliament (Article 120)

Official languages for transaction of business are Hindi and English. Presiding Officer may permit a member to address in their mother tongue. Simultaneous translation available for 8th Schedule languages. English was continued beyond 1965 by Official Languages Act, 1963.

II. Functional Classification of Bills (By Purpose)

  • Repeal Bills (खारिज करना): Passed specifically to abolish or nullify an existing statute (e.g., Farm Laws Repeal Act, 2021).
  • Merger Bills: Combines multiple existing legislative enactments into a unified, consolidated code (e.g., The 4 Labour Codes consolidating 29 labour laws).
  • Amending Laws: Modifies, adds, or deletes specific clauses in existing statutory or constitutional provisions.
  • Renewal Laws: Extends the operational validity or quota period of an expiring statute (e.g., 104th CAA, 2019 extending SC/ST reservation in legislative assemblies for 10 years).

Stage Details & Process Notes:

  • Stage I (Pre-Parliamentary): 1st draft prepared by Secretary / Committee / Experts / Lawyers → Revised by Law Ministry → Sent to concerned Ministry for introduction.
  • Stage II (Parliamentary): 1st Reading: Motion for leave to introduce bill; 2nd Reading: Clause-by-clause scrutiny & amendments; 3rd Reading: Direct voting (Yes/No) with NO further clause amendments permitted.

  • Note: Financial Bills follow the exact same 3-stage process as Ordinary Bills.

IV. Joint Sitting of Parliament (Article 108)

Summoned by President to break legislative deadlocks (if > 6 months elapse excluding adjournments/prorogations > 4 days).

Presiding Order: 1) Speaker → 2) Deputy Speaker → 3) Deputy Chairman of RS → 4) Member chosen by sitting. (RS Chairman NEVER presides). Simple majority of total combined members present and voting required.

Historic Instances (Invoked 3 Times Only):

  1. Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1961
  2. Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1978
  3. Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002


6. Comprehensive Parliamentary Motions, Devices & Resolutions

I. Functional Classification of Motions

  • Substantive Motion: Self-contained, independent proposal on major matters (e.g., Impeachment of President, removal of CJI).
  • Substitute Motion: Moved in substitution of original motion; if passed, completely supersedes original.
  • Subsidiary Motion: Depends on main motion. Divided into: 1) *Ancillary* (regular procedural step); 2) *Superseding* (replaces during debate); 3) *Amendment* (modifies part).

II. Types of Closure Motions

  • Simple Closure: Moved to put matter to immediate vote as debate has been sufficient.
  • Closure by Compartments: Clauses grouped into compartments; voting done on whole group.
  • Kangaroo Closure: Only important clauses discussed and voted; minor ones skipped.
  • Guillotine Closure: All undiscussed clauses put to vote together due to time constraint on last day of debate.

III. Censure Motion vs. No-Confidence Motion

Feature Censure Motion No-Confidence Motion
Specific Reason MUST state specific grounds/policies criticized NO reasons required
Target Individual minister, group, or full Cabinet Entire Council of Ministers ONLY
House & Support Lok Sabha ONLY Lok Sabha ONLY (Requires 50 members support)
Consequence of Passing Govt NOT required to resign, but must prove majority Council of Ministers MUST resign

IV. Other Essential Parliamentary Devices

  • Adjournment Motion: Draws attention to a definite matter of urgent public importance. Interruption of normal business for min 2.5 hours. Needs 50 members' support. Lok Sabha ONLY (involves censure).
  • Calling Attention Motion: Indian innovation (1954). Member seeks official statement from Minister on urgent public issue. Mentioned in Rules of Procedure.
  • Point of Order: Raised when proceedings do not follow House rules. Suspends normal proceedings; NO debate allowed. Decision of Presiding Officer is final.
  • Half-an-Hour Discussion: Clarifies factual matters of public importance; 3 days/week; no formal motion or vote.
  • Short Duration Discussion (2-Hour): Urgent matters; 2 days/week; no formal motion or vote (In place since 1953).
  • Special Mention / Rule 377: Raised matters not covered elsewhere. Termed *Special Mention* in RS, *Rule 377 Mention* in LS.

V. Motions vs. Resolutions

Every resolution is a substantive motion, but not all motions are resolutions. Resolutions require formal voting. Classified into: 1) Private Member's Resolution (Discussed on alternate Fridays); 2) Government Resolution; 3) Statutory Resolution (Pursuant to Constitution/Act).



7. Budgetary Procedure, Charged Expenditure & Funds

I. History & 6 Stages of Passing the Budget (Article 112)

Budget is termed Annual Financial Statement under Article 112. Railway Budget (separated in 1924 via Acworth Committee 1921) was re-merged into Union Budget in 2017. Presented on 1st February.

💰 Visual Roadmap: 6 Stages of Union Budget Approval

flowchart LR B1["1. Presentation"] --> B2["2. Discussion"] --> B3["3. DRSC Audit"] --> B4["4. Voting"] --> B5["5. Appropriation"] --> B6["6. Finance Bill"]
  1. Presentation: Finance Minister delivers Budget Speech in Lok Sabha; laid in Rajya Sabha. Economic Survey presented 1–2 days prior.
  2. General Discussion: Held in both Houses; no cut motions or voting.
  3. Scrutiny by DRSCs: Houses adjourn for 3–4 weeks while 24 DRSCs examine Demands for Grants.
  4. Voting on Demands for Grants: Lok Sabha EXCLUSIVE Power. Remaining demands voted on last day via **Guillotine**.
  5. Passing of Appropriation Bill (Article 114): Authorizes withdrawal of money from Consolidated Fund of India. No amendments allowed.
  6. Passing of Finance Bill: Legalizes taxation proposals. Money Bill processed within 75 days (Provisional Collection of Taxes Act 1931).

II. Charged Expenditure (Non-Votable - Article 112(3))

Can be discussed in Parliament, but CANNOT be voted upon. Includes: Salaries/Pensions of President, Presiding Officers, SC Judges, CAG, UPSC Members, HC Judges pensions, Debt charges, and Court decrees against GoI.

III. Cut Motions & Types of Grants

Cut Motions (Lok Sabha Only):

  • Policy Cut: Expresses policy disapproval; reduces demand to Re 1.
  • Economy Cut: Suggests administrative economy; reduces demand by a specific amount.
  • Token Cut: Vents specific grievance; reduces demand by Rs 100.

Special Grants (Outside Regular Budget - Article 115 & 116):

  • Supplementary Grant: Allocated amount found insufficient during current FY.
  • Additional Grant: Expenditure for a new service not contemplated in budget.
  • Excess Grant: Expenditure exceeding budget grant (Requires PAC approval first).
  • Vote of Credit: Blank check granted for unexpected/indefinite demand (e.g., war emergency).
  • Exceptional Grant: For special purposes outside current year's routine services.
  • Token Grant: Re-appropriation of funds from one head to another (Votes nominal Rs 100).

IV. Government Funds Comparison

Fund Article Nature & Operation
Consolidated Fund of India Article 266(1) All revenues, loans, recoveries. Withdrawn ONLY via **Appropriation Act** (Parliamentary Law).
Public Account of India Article 266(2) Provident funds, savings deposits, judicial deposits. Operated by **Executive Action** alone.
Contingency Fund of India Article 267 Rs 30,000 Cr corpus. Placed at disposal of President for unforeseen emergencies (operated by Finance Secretary).


8. Parliamentary Privileges & Contempt of House

I. Privileges Categorized (Articles 105 & 194)

Collective Privileges: Right to publish/exclude debates (Press reports protected by 44th CAA 1978); Right to hold secret sittings; Power to punish members/outsiders for breach or contempt (Reprimand, suspension, expulsion, imprisonment); Exclude courts from inquiring into House proceedings (Art 122).

Individual Privileges: Absolute freedom of speech floor of House; Immunity from civil arrest 40 days before & after session (NO immunity from criminal arrest or preventive detention); Exemption from jury service while session is active.

II. Breach of Privilege vs. Contempt of House

Aspect Breach of Privilege Contempt of House
Scope Violation of a specific codified/established privilege Broader category: Actions that obstruct or cause disrespect
Relationship Every breach of privilege is a contempt Contempt can occur without violating a specific privilege

Sources of Privileges: Constitutional provisions (Art 105), Parliamentary Laws, Rules of Both Houses, Conventions, Judicial Interpretations. Privileges remain uncodified.



9. Parliamentary Committees & IPG Group

I. Financial Standing Committees (UPSC High-Yield)

Committee Members & Breakdown Chairperson Convention Key Features & Restrictions
Public Accounts Committee (PAC) 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) Appointed by Speaker; From Opposition since 1967 Examines CAG audit reports. CAG acts as "Guide, Friend & Philosopher". Ministers ineligible.
Estimates Committee 30 (Lok Sabha ONLY) Appointed by Speaker; Invariably from **Ruling Party** Continuous Economy Committee. Examines budget estimates post-vote. Largest committee.
Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU) 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) Appointed by Speaker; From Lok Sabha members only Established 1964 (Krishna Menon Committee). Examines PSU accounts & CAG reports.

II. Departmental Related Standing Committees (DRCs) & Other Bodies

  • DRSCs: Total **24 Committees** (16 LS, 8 RS). 31 members each (21 LS, 10 RS). Nominated annually. Scrutinize DRGs, Bills, & Annual Reports.
  • Inquiry Committees: Committee on Petitions (15 LS, 10 RS), Committee of Privileges (15 LS, 10 RS), Ethics Committee (15 LS, 10 RS).
  • Scrutiny Committees: Govt Assurances (15 LS, 10 RS), Subordinate Legislation (15 each), SC/ST Welfare (20 LS, 10 RS), Women Empowerment (20 LS, 10 RS).
  • Day-to-Day Committees: Business Advisory Committee (15 LS headed by Speaker, 11 RS headed by Chairman), Rules Committee.
  • Consultative Committees: Non-parliamentary committees attached to Ministries chaired by Ministers (Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs guidelines).

III. Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)

Autonomous body established in 1949. Serves as National Group of **Inter-Parliamentary Union (IPU)** and India Branch of **Commonwealth Parliamentary Association (CPA)**.

Office Bearers: Ex-officio President = Speaker of Lok Sabha; Vice-Presidents = Deputy Speaker LS & Deputy Chairman RS; Secretary-General = Secretary-General of Lok Sabha.



10. Position, Special Powers & Comparative Study of Rajya Sabha

I. Exclusive Special Powers of Rajya Sabha

  1. Article 249: Authorizes Parliament to legislate on a State List item in national interest via resolution passed by 2/3rd members present and voting (Valid for 1 year at a time).
  2. Article 312: Authorizes creation of one or more **All-India Services (AIS)** common to Union & States via 2/3rd majority.
  3. Article 67: Resolution for removal of Vice-President MUST originate in Rajya Sabha (Effective majority in RS + Simple majority in LS).
  4. Proclamation Approval during LS Dissolution (Arts 352/356/360): Approves Emergency proclamations if Lok Sabha stands dissolved.
