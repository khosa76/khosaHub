CHAPTER 01

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND & MAKING OF CONSTITUTION

Company Rule (1773–1858), Crown Rule (1858–1947), Cabinet Mission Plan 1946, Committees, and Drafting of the Indian Constitution

📖 Chapter Index

1. Company Rule (1773 – 1858) 2. Crown Rule (1858 – 1947) 3. Making of the Constitution (1946-1950) 4. Committees & Drafting Committee

1. Company Rule (1773 – 1858)


  • Regulating Act of 1773:
    • Ended Clive's dual system of Bengal; 1st step to regulate East India Company's administration.
    • Designated Governor of Bengal as Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings was the first).
    • Established Supreme Court of Judicature at Fort William, Calcutta (1774).
    • Prohibited Company servants from engaging in private trade.
  • Pitt's India Act of 1784:
    • Dual Control: Separated commercial functions (Court of Directors) and political functions (Board of Control).
    • Company's territories first called "British possessions in India".
  • Charter Act of 1793:
    • Renewed commercial privileges for 20 years. Board of Control paid out of Indian revenues (continued till 1919 Act). Separated revenue and judicial functions.
  • Charter Act of 1813:
    • Explicitly defined Crown's sovereignty over British territories in India.
    • Ended Company's trade monopoly except for tea and trade with China.
    • Allowed Christian missionaries; allocated ₹1 lakh annually for education.
  • Charter Act of 1833:
    • Complete centralisation: Governor-General of Bengal became Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck was the first).
    • Ended Company's trade monopoly completely; EIC became a purely administrative body.
    • First Law Commission set up under Lord Macaulay for law codification. Prohibited slavery in British India.
  • Charter Act of 1853:
    • Separated executive and legislative functions of the Governor-General's Council.
    • Introduced open competition for civil services (Macaulay Committee 1854).
    • Introduced local representation in the Indian Central Legislative Council.


2. Crown Rule (1858 – 1947)


  • Government of India Act 1858 (Act for Better Government of India):
    • Abolished EIC; transferred power directly to the British Crown.
    • Changed Governor-General designation to Viceroy of India (Lord Canning was the first).
    • Created new office of Secretary of State for India, assisted by a 15-member Council of India.
  • Indian Councils Act of 1861:
    • Introduced representative institutions (Viceroy nominated non-official Indians: Raja of Benares, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao).
    • Portfolio System legalized (introduced by Lord Canning in 1859).
    • Empowered Viceroy to issue ordinances (6 months validity). Restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras.
  • Indian Councils Act of 1892:
    • Increased non-official members; gave legislative councils power to discuss the budget and address questions.
  • Indian Councils Act of 1909 (Morley-Minto Reforms):
    • Introduced communal electorate for Muslims (Lord Minto known as "Father of Communal Electorate").
    • First Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council: Satyendra Prasad Sinha (Law Member).
  • Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms):
    • Introduced Dyarchy in Provinces (Transferred Subjects vs. Reserved Subjects).
    • Introduced Bicameralism & Direct Elections at the Centre.
    • Central Public Service Commission set up in 1926 (Lee Commission 1924). Extended communal electorates to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans.
  • Government of India Act 1935:
    • Provided for an All-India Federation (never materialized).
    • Divided powers into 3 Lists: Federal List (59 items), Provincial List (54 items), Concurrent List (36 items).
    • Abolished Dyarchy in Provinces & introduced Provincial Autonomy; introduced Dyarchy at Centre.
    • Established Federal Court (1937) and Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
  • Indian Independence Act 1947:
    • Ended British rule; created two independent dominions: India and Pakistan.
    • Lord Mountbatten became the 1st Governor-General of independent India; C. Rajagopalachari became the 1st Indian Governor-General.


3. Making of the Constitution (1946 – 1950)


  • Idea & Demand: First proposed by M.N. Roy in 1934; officially demanded by INC in 1935. Accepted in principle by British in August Offer 1940 & Cripps Proposal 1942.
  • Cabinet Mission Plan (1946): Constituent Assembly constituted in November 1946. Total strength: 389 seats (296 British India + 93 Princely States).
  • First Meeting: December 9, 1946 (Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha interim President). On Dec 11, 1946, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected President; H.C. Mukherjee & V.T. Krishnamachari Vice-Presidents.
  • Objective Resolution: Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on December 13, 1946; unanimously adopted on January 22, 1947. Formed the foundation of the Preamble.
  • Key Milestones: Enacted & adopted on November 26, 1949 (Constitution Day). Came into full force on January 26, 1950 (Republic Day). Took 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days.

Drafting Committee (Constituted Aug 29, 1947)

7 Members of the Drafting Committee:

  1. 1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman) — Father of the Indian Constitution
  2. 2. N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar
  3. 3. Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
  4. 4. Dr. K.M. Munshi
  5. 5. Syed Mohammad Saadullah
  6. 6. N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter due to ill health)
  7. 7. T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan who died in 1948)
