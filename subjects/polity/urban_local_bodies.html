1. Historical Evolution & 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992


Constitutional Framework & Central Placement:

  • State List Placement: "Local Government" is listed in the State List (7th Schedule) of the Constitution of India.
  • 3 Central Ministries Dealing with ULBs:
    1. Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs: General urban local governance.
    2. Ministry of Defence: Cantonment Boards governance.
    3. Ministry of Home Affairs: Union Territories urban bodies.
  • 74th CAA 1992 Milestones: Introduced under P.V. Narasimha Rao Govt (Passed Dec 1992, Presidential Assent April 1993, Enforced 1 June 1993). Added Part IX-A (Articles 243-P to 243-ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule (18 Functional Items under Art 243-W).

Historical Milestones of Urban Governance in India

  • 1687–1688: Charter issued on Dec 30, 1687 for establishment of the first municipal corporation in India at Madras (Chennai), formally set up on Sep 29, 1688.
  • 1726: Municipal Corporations set up in Bombay and Calcutta.
  • 1870: Lord Mayo’s Resolution on financial decentralization.
  • 1882: Lord Ripon’s Resolution on local self-government, known as the 'Magna Carta of Local Self-Government'. Lord Ripon is regarded as the "Father of Local Self-Government in India".
  • 1907–1909: Royal Commission on Decentralisation (chaired by C.E.H. Hobhouse).
  • 1919 & 1935: GOI Act 1919 made local self-govt a "Transferred Subject" under Dyarchy; GOI Act 1935 made it a Provincial Subject.
  • Key Committees: P.K. Wattal, John Matthai, Nur-ud-din Ahmed, A.P. Jain (1963–66 - recommended merging small town committees with PRIs), Rafiq Zakaria, B.S. Murthy, Girijapati Mukharji, K.N. Sahaya, C.M. Correa.

Three Types of Municipalities

Category Applicability & Territory Type
Nagar Panchayat For a Transitional Area (moving from rural to urban)
Municipal Council For a Smaller Urban Area
Municipal Corporation For a Larger Urban Area

Industrial Township Exception: If municipal services in an urban area are provided by an industrial establishment, the Governor may declare it an Industrial Township; no municipality needs to be constituted.


Composition, Wards Committees & Reservations

  • Direct Elections: All members of a municipality are directly elected by voters from electoral wards. State legislature may provide for non-voting members with special knowledge in municipal administration.
  • Wards Committees (Article 243-S): Mandatory in every municipality having a population of 3 Lakhs or more.
  • Reservation for SCs/STs: Reserved in proportion to their population in the municipal area. SC/ST reservation limit under Article 334 is 80 years (up to 2030).
  • Reservation for Women: At least 1/3rd of total seats reserved for women (including women from SC/ST categories). State legislatures may also reserve chairperson posts for SCs, STs, Women, or OBCs.

Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W) – 18 Functions / Functional Domains

  • 1. Urban planning including town planning.
  • 2. Regulation of land use and construction of buildings.
  • 3. Planning for economic and social development.
  • 4. Roads and bridges.
  • 5. Water supply for domestic, industrial, and commercial purposes.
  • 6. Public health, sanitation, conservancy, and solid waste management.
  • 7. Fire services.
  • 8. Urban forestry, environmental protection, and ecological promotion.
  • 9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections of society (handicapped/mentally retarded).
  • 10. Slum improvement and upgradation.
  • 11. Urban poverty alleviation.
  • 12. Provision of urban amenities (parks, gardens, playgrounds).
  • 13. Promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects.
  • 14. Burials, burial grounds, cremations, cremation grounds, and electric crematoriums.
  • 15. Cattle ponds, prevention of cruelty to animals.
  • 16. Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths.
  • 17. Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, and public conveniences.
  • 18. Regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries.

4. 8 Types of Urban Local Bodies in India


  1. Municipal Corporation: Set up in big cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, etc.) by State Acts or Parliament. 3 Wings: (1) The Council (deliberative, headed by Mayor - 1 yr term), (2) Standing Committees, (3) Municipal Commissioner (Executive head, usually IAS officer).
  2. Municipality: Set up for towns and smaller cities. Headed by President/Chairman (who holds substantial executive power) and a Chief Executive Officer (CEO).
  3. Notified Area Committee: Created by state gazette notification for rapidly growing industrial towns. Entirely nominated by state govt; neither a statutory nor an elected body.
  4. Town Area Committee: Semi-municipal authority for small towns created by separate state act; handles limited civic duties (drainage, lighting).
  5. Cantonment Board: Administers civilian populations in military cantonments under the Cantonments Act, 2006 (Ministry of Defence). Currently 62 Cantonment Boards exist in 4 population categories.
    Composition of a Category I Board: Station Commander (Ex-officio President), Executive Officer (appointed by President of India), 3 Military Officers, 1 First Class Magistrate, and 8 elected members (VP elected among them for 5 years).
  6. Township: Established by large public enterprises for housing colonies; managed by a Town Administrator without elected representatives.
  7. Port Trust: Established by Act of Parliament in major port cities (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) to manage ports and civic amenities.
  8. Special Purpose Agency: Function-based statutory bodies (Urban Development Authorities, Housing Boards, Water Supply & Sewerage Boards).

5. Personnel Systems, Revenue Sources & Central Council


3 Municipal Personnel Systems:

  • 1. Separate Personnel System: Each local body independently appoints and manages its own non-transferable staff (most common).
  • 2. Unified Personnel System: State-wide cadre created by state govt; officers transferable between urban local bodies.
  • 3. Integrated Personnel System: Personnel are part of state civil services; transferable between local bodies and state departments.

Sources of Municipal Revenue

  • Tax Revenue: Property tax (largest source), entertainment tax, advertisement tax, professional tax, tolls, and taxes on animals, water, and lighting.
  • Non-Tax Revenue: User charges (water, sanitation, sewerage), fees, fines, royalties, dividends, and interest.
  • Grants-in-Aid & Devolution: Funds transferred by State Government based on State Finance Commission recommendations for infrastructure and development.
  • Loans: Raised from state government or financial institutions (Requires state government approval for borrowing).

Central Council of Local Government (Article 263):

Established in 1954 under Article 263 of the Constitution. Since 1958, it deals exclusively with urban local governance. Chaired by the Union Minister for Housing and Urban Affairs alongside State Ministers for local self-government.
