## SECTION 2: BASIC FACTS & CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS

### Basic Facts About Parliament



1. It is a legislative organ.


2. India follows representative democracy.


3. MPs are elected from all over India to represent people in Parliament.


4. Law-making is one of the functions of the Legislature, but not the only one. It is the center of all democratic political processes.



---

### Constitutional Provisions



* **Part V** of the Constitution deals with Parliament (**Articles 79 to 122**).


* **Article 79:** Mandates that there shall be a Parliament consisting of the President and two Houses:


* House of the People (Lok Sabha)


* Council of States (Rajya Sabha)




* **Article 80:** Talks about the composition of Rajya Sabha.


* **Article 81:** Talks about the composition of Lok Sabha.

## SECTION 3: HISTORY & HOUSE COMPARISONS

### History of Parliament



* The British influenced India to become a parliamentary democracy.


* They built our Parliament in New Delhi on Raisina Hill. The Chief Architect of the Parliament was **Sir Edwin Lutyens**.


* **In 1954**, the Hindi names **"Rajya Sabha"** and **"Lok Sabha"** were adopted by Parliament instead of the English names "Council of States" and "House of the People".



---

### The Two Houses Defined



* **Lok Sabha:** Lower House / Popular House / 1st Chamber. It represents the people of India as a whole.


* **Rajya Sabha:** Upper House / 2nd Chamber / House of Elders. It represents the States & UTs of the Indian Union.

## SECTION 4: DETAILED COMPOSITION OF HOUSES

### 1. Rajya Sabha (Article 80)



1. **Max Sanctioned Strength:** 250


2. **Total Member Strength:** 245


3. **Representatives of States:** 229 *(Mentioned in the 4th Schedule)*

4. **Representatives of UTs:** 4


5. **Members Nominated by President:** 12



#### Key Notes on Rajya Sabha:



* Representatives of each UT in Rajya Sabha are indirectly elected by members of an electoral college specifically constituted for that purpose.


* Representatives of States are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assembly (MLAs).


* Out of 8 UTs, **only Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu & Kashmir** have representation in Rajya Sabha. The other 5 UTs have populations too small to have representation.


* **Nominated Members:** 12 members are nominated by the President of India from people having special knowledge or practical experience in: **Art, Literature, Science, Social Service**.



---

### 2. Lok Sabha (Article 81)



1. **Max Sanctioned Strength:** 552


2. **Total Member Strength:** 543


3. **Representatives of States:** 530


4. **Representatives of UTs:** 13


5. **Anglo-Indian Nominated Seats:** 2 *(Terminated by the 104th Amendment Act, 2019)*.



#### Key Notes on Lok Sabha:



* Elections to Lok Sabha are based on the principle of **Universal Adult Franchise**. Every citizen aged 18 years and above can vote.


* The Constitution empowers Parliament to prescribe the manner of choosing representatives of UTs in Lok Sabha. For this, Parliament enacted the **UTs (Direct Election to the House of the People) Act, 1965**, making UT representatives directly elected by the people.



---

### Legal Definitions & Acts



* **Un-Constitutional:** Declared unconstitutional under Articles 13, 32, or 226 because it falls outside the scope of the Constitution.


* **Non-Constitutional:** Not written in the Constitution, but legal by an Act of Parliament (e.g., RBI, LIC).


* **Extra-Constitutional:** Concept derived/expanded outside the text (e.g., National Commission for Women).

## SECTION 5: TYPES OF ELECTORAL SYSTEMS

### 1. First-Past-The-Post (FPTP)



* Used for electing MPs (Lok Sabha), MLAs (State Assemblies), and PRI / Municipal Corporation members.


* Also called the **Plurality System**.


* Under this system, whichever candidate gets more votes than any other candidate is declared elected. The winning candidate does **not** need to secure an absolute majority (50% + 1).



#### Merits of FPTP

:

1. Simplicity


2. Stability


3. Freedom to choose candidates directly


4. Broad-based participation



#### Demerits of FPTP

:

1. Exclusion of small parties


2. Encourages politics based on caste, religion, ethnicity


3. Distortion of electoral outcome (a party with a majority of votes may not get a majority of seats)



---

### 2. Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV)



* Used for indirect elections: President of India, Vice-President of India, Rajya Sabha, and State Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishad).


* A party/candidate gets representation in proportion to the votes secured. Needs a specific quota/percentage of votes to win.


* Cannot be applied to mass elections due to complexity.

## SECTION 7: SESSIONS OF PARLIAMENT & TERMINATIONS

### Constitutional Provisions on Sessions



* **Article 85:** The President of India summons each House of Parliament from time to time to meet.


* **Gap Limit:** Parliament must meet at least twice a year. The maximum gap between two sessions **cannot exceed 6 months**.


* **3 Annual Sessions in Practice:**

1. **Budget Session:** February to May (Longest session).


2. **Monsoon Session:** July to September.


3. **Winter Session:** November to December.





---

### Important Session Terminology



* **Session of Parliament:** Period between the first sitting of the House and its prorogation (or dissolution).


* **Recess:** Period between the prorogation of the House and its reassembly in a new session.



---

### Differences: Dissolution vs. Prorogation vs. Adjournment



| Feature | Dissolution

 | Prorogation

 | Adjournment

 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | End of the life of Lok Sabha.

 |
