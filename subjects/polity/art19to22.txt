### Timeline Diagram Text: Right to Privacy

`1948` ──► `1954` ──► `1962` ──► `1957` ──► `2003` ──► `2017`

* **1948 (Constituent Assembly debate):** Kazi Syed Karimuddin (Member of Constituent Assembly) drafted a resolution for a Right to Privacy because of CrPC (Criminal Procedure Code), but Dr. B.R. Ambedkar refused to dedicate.
* **1954 (M.P. Sharma case - Dalmia group of companies):** Supreme Court refused to acknowledge the Right to Privacy.
* **1962 (Kharak Singh case):** Supreme Court refused.
* **1957 (Govind v. State of M.P.):** Supreme Court gave NO relief in terms of Privacy; the police were only warned.
* **2003 (Surjit Singh Thind case):** Virginity test was considered illegal & element of privacy was recognized.
* **2017 (K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar case) v. Union of India):** The question before the court was: *Whether Aadhaar is constitutional or not?* The Supreme Court held that the Right to Privacy is an implied Fundamental Right under Article 21.

#### Landmark Case Note:

In the landmark case of **Maneka Gandhi**, the Supreme Court held that:

1. Article 14 & 19 are interlinked with Article 21.
2. **Golden Triangle** constitutes Article 14, 19, and 21.
3. From now on, the Supreme Court will also focus on **Due Process of Law**.

### Scope of Article 21 (MAINS)

*(Important topic for Mains)*

1. **Right to Live with Human Dignity:**
* → Right to clean environment
* → Surrogacy
* → Abortion
* → Legalization of LGBTQ Marriage
* **Navtej Singh Johar case (2018/2019):** Section 377 of IPC was repealed which criminalized unnatural sex *(British de zamane da law)*.
* **Joseph Shine case:** Supreme Court held that Adultery (physical relation of a married person with somebody who is not their wife/husband) under Section 497 of IPC is no longer valid.


2. **Right to Livelihood:**
* **Olga Tellis Case:** Famously called the Slum Dweller Case. Supreme Court held that people living in slums or on government property is not trespassing (going onto somebody's land/property without permission); they are entitled to appropriate resettlement.


3. **Right to Health:**
* **Parmanand Katara Case:** Supreme Court recognized the right to health by ordering all doctors to extend medical assistance to the patient immediately without legal formalities.


4. **Right to Die:**
* **P. Rathinam Case:** Section 309 of IPC (attempt to suicide) was held void.
* **Gian Kaur Case:** Supreme Court overruled the *P. Rathinam* judgment, but in 2017 the Mental Healthcare Act made it possible.
* **Aruna Shanbaug Case:** Supreme Court allowed Passive Euthanasia.
* **Common Cause Case:** Article 21 includes the right to die with dignity (Passive Euthanasia).


5. **Right to Sleep:**
* **Ramlila Maidan Case:** Supreme Court recognized that every citizen has the right to sleep comfortably & freely.


6. **Right to Clean Environment:**
* **M.C. Mehta Case**

## Article 20

No person shall face these 3 things:

1. **No Retrospective (Pichhe / Pehla toh) Application of Law:**
In India, no person can be held guilty if on the date of the crime there is no law regarding that crime. *(Note: This is not applicable to Civil Law).*
2. **No Double Jeopardy:**
No person can be punished for the same offence twice.
3. **Right against Self-Incrimination:**
No person can be forced to testify against himself.
*Exception:* NARCO Test.

---

## Article 22

Article 22 protects the accused or suspect.

1. **Reasons for Arrest:** Any police officer who is arresting a suspect shall provide the reason for arrest to the suspect or any other person who is available at the time of arrest.
2. **24-Hours Rule:** A person who is arrested shall be produced before the court of the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding travel time.
3. **Exceptions:**
* Alien Enemy
* Preventive Detention Laws (e.g., NSA, AFSPA)
