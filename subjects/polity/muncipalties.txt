Urban Local Government in India
Constitutional Status
● Urban local government was constitutionalised through the 74th Constitutional Amendment
Act, 1992.
● This provided a uniform structure, legal status, and powers to urban bodies.
Ministries Dealing with Urban Local Bodies
At the central level, three ministries are involved depending on the area:
1. Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs
– General urban local governance
2. Ministry of Defence
– In case of Cantonment Boards
3. Ministry of Home Affairs
– In case of Union Territories
Constitutional Placement
● "Local Government" is listed under the State List of the 7th Schedule of the Constitution.
● Hence, states have the primary authority to legislate on urban local governance.
Evolution of Urban Local Bodies
1. 1687–1688:
○ Charter issued on 30 December 1687 for establishment of the first municipal corporation in
India at Madras (now Chennai).
○ Formally established on 29 September 1688.
2. 1726:
○ Municipal Corporations set up in Bombay and Calcutta.
3. 1870:
○ Lord Mayo’s Resolution on financial decentralisation laid the foundation for urban local
self-governance.
4. 1882:
○ Lord Ripon’s Resolution on local self-government.
○ Known as the 'Magna Carta of local self-government'.
○ Lord Ripon is regarded as the "Father of Local Self-Government in India".
5. 1907–1909:
○ Royal Commission on Decentralisation under the chairmanship of C.E.H. Hobhouse.
○ Recommended strengthening local bodies.
6. 1919:
○ Under the Government of India Act, 1919, local self-government became a "transferred
subject" under the control of Indian ministers (dyarchy system).
7. 1924:
○ Cantonments Act was passed by the Central Legislature to regulate governance in
military cantonments.
8. 1935:
○ Under the Government of India Act, 1935, local self-government became a provincial
subject, allowing states/provinces to frame their own legislation on local bodies.

74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992
Key Features
1. New Part IX-A Added
○ Title: The Municipalities
○ Articles 243-P to 243-ZG
2. New Twelfth Schedule Added
○ Contains 18 functional items under Article 243-W.
○ These define the responsibilities and domain of municipalities (e.g., urban planning, public
health, roads, water supply).
3. Constitutional Status
○ Municipalities became justiciable entities under the Constitution.
○ States are now constitutionally bound to establish and empower municipal bodies.
4. Obligatory Nature
○ States cannot deny the formation of ULBs or delay elections.
○ ULBs must function according to the framework provided in the Act.

The Governor classifies urban areas into transitional, small, or large based on:

3. Composition of Municipalities
● All members of a municipality are to be directly elected by the people.
● Each municipal area is divided into wards for elections.
Other Representatives in the Municipality (Non-voting or State-legislated members):
1. Persons with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration (no voting rights)
2. Lok Sabha and State Assembly members representing the municipal area
3. Rajya Sabha and Legislative Council members registered as electors in the area
4. Chairpersons of Committees (except Wards Committees)
4. Wards Committees
● Mandatory in municipalities with population ≥ 3 lakhs
● Composed of one or more wards
● State legislature can define:
○ Composition
○ Area covered
○ Method of filling seats
5. Other Committees
● State legislatures may provide for the constitution of other municipal committees.
● Chairpersons of such committees can be made members of the municipality.
6. Reservation of Seats
For Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs):
● Seats reserved in proportion to their population in the municipal area.
For Women:
● At least one-third of the total seats reserved for women, including women from SC/ST categories.
For Chairperson Posts:
● State legislature may reserve chairperson positions for:
○ SCs
○ STs
○ Women
○ Backward Classes (optional)
Time Limit (Article 334):
● Reservation for SCs/STs in seats and chairperson posts will cease after 80 years from the
commencement of the Constitution, i.e., after 2030.

Powers and Functions of Municipalities
The State Legislature may devolve powers to municipalities to enable them to function as institutions of
self-government.
This includes:
1. Preparation of plans for:
○ Economic development
○ Social justice
2. Implementation of schemes related to the above objectives.
● The functions may include subjects listed in the Twelfth Schedule, which contains 18 functional
items, such as:
○ Urban planning, water supply, sanitation, slum improvement, public health, education, etc.
Finances of Municipalities - Same
Audit of Accounts - Same
Application to Union Territories - Same
Example:
● In 2001, the President directed that Articles 243ZD (District Planning Committee) and 243ZE
(Metropolitan Planning Committee) shall not apply to Delhi.
Exempted Areas
The Act does not apply to:
1. Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas.
2. Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council in West Bengal.
Parliament may extend these provisions to such areas with suitable modifications.
District Planning Committee (DPC) – Article 243ZD
Purpose:
To consolidate plans prepared by Panchayats and Municipalities and formulate a district-level
development plan.
Provisions:
● Composition:
○ 4/5th of members to be elected by and from elected members of:
■ District Panchayats
■ Municipalities
○ In proportion to rural and urban population of the district.
● State Legislature may decide:
○ Structure
○ Election method
○ Functions
○ Election of Chairperson
Functions of DPC:
While preparing the district development plan, the DPC shall consider:
1. Common interests between Panchayats and Municipalities:
○ Spatial planning
○ Water and resource sharing
○ Infrastructure integration
○ Environmental conservation
2. Available resources (financial and other)
3. Consultations:
○ With institutions/organisations as specified by the Governor
● Chairperson of the DPC shall forward the development plan to the State Government.
Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC) – Article 243ZE
Applicability:
● Every metropolitan area must have a Metropolitan Planning Committee (MPC).
Metropolitan Area means:
● Area with a population of 10 lakh or more,
● Spread over one or more districts,
● Containing two or more municipalities and/or panchayats,
● Or other contiguous urban/rural areas.
Functions:
To prepare a draft development plan for the metropolitan area.
State Legislature May Provide for:
1. Composition of the committee.
2. Manner of election of committee members.
3. Representation of:
○ Central Government,
○ State Government,
○ Other relevant organisations.
4. Functions of the MPC relating to planning and coordination.
5. Manner of election of the Chairperson.
Composition of MPC:
● 2/3rd members elected by:
○ Elected members of municipalities and
○ Chairpersons of panchayats in the metropolitan area,
● From amongst themselves.
Representation in proportion to the population of municipalities and panchayats in the area.
In Draft Planning, MPC Must Consider:
1. Municipal and Panchayat plans within the metro area.
2. Matters of common interest such as:
○ Spatial planning,
○ Shared resources (e.g., water),
○ Infrastructure,
○ Environmental protection.
3. National and State-level objectives and priorities.
4. Likely investments by:
○ Central Government agencies,
○ State agencies,
○ Other available financial/physical resources.
● Consultations with institutions as Governor may specify.
● Chairperson shall forward the draft plan to the state government.

Twelfth Schedule – 18 Functions of Municipalities
The Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W) includes the following functional domains of municipalities:
1. Urban planning including town planning.
2. Regulation of land use and construction of buildings.
3. Planning for economic and social development.
4. Roads and bridges.
5. Water supply for domestic, industrial, and commercial purposes.
6. Public health, sanitation, conservancy, and solid waste management.
7. Fire services.
8. Urban forestry, environmental protection, and ecological promotion.
9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections of society, including the handicapped and mentally
retarded
10. Slum improvement and upgradation
11. Urban poverty alleviation
12. Provision of urban amenities and facilities such as parks, gardens, playgrounds
13. Promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects
14. Burials and burial grounds, cremations and cremation grounds, and electric crematoriums
15. Cattle ponds, prevention of cruelty to animals
16. Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths
17. Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops, and public conveniences
18. Regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries
Types of Urban Governments in India
There are eight types of urban local bodies created for administration of urban areas:
1. Municipal Corporation
2. Municipality
3. Notified Area Committee
4. Town Area Committee
5. Cantonment Board
6. Township
7. Port Trust
8. Special Purpose Agency
1. Municipal Corporation
● Established in big cities such as Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, and Bangalore.
● Created by state legislature acts (for states) or by Parliament (for union territories).
● There may be a common act for all corporations in a state or separate ones.
Authorities within a Municipal Corporation:
1. The Council
○ Legislative and deliberative body.
○ Composed of directly elected councillors and nominated experts.
○ Reservation for SCs, STs, and women is ensured under the 74th Amendment.
○ Headed by a Mayor (mainly ceremonial, presides over meetings), assisted by Deputy Mayor.
○ Mayor usually elected for a 1-year renewable term.
2. Standing Committees
○ Handle specific functional areas like education, health, public works, finance, and taxation.
○ Created for efficient division of work within the council.
3. Municipal Commissioner
○ Appointed by the state government.
○ Usually an IAS officer.
○ Acts as the executive head of the corporation.
○ Responsible for executing decisions of the council and committees.
2. Municipality
● Municipalities are formed for the governance of towns and smaller cities.
● They are created by acts of state legislatures (for states) or acts of Parliament (for union
territories).
● They are known by different names such as municipal council, municipal committee, municipal
board, borough municipality, or city municipality.
Authorities of a Municipality:
1. The Council:
○ Deliberative and legislative wing.
○ Comprised of directly elected councillors.
○ Headed by a President/Chairman, assisted by a Vice-President/Vice-Chairman.
○ Unlike the Mayor (who is mostly ceremonial), the Chairman plays a significant executive
role.
2. Standing Committees:
○ Handle sectors like taxation, finance, public works, and health.
○ Facilitate specialized governance.
3. Chief Executive Officer (CEO) / Chief Municipal Officer:
○ Handles general administration.
○ Appointed by the state government.
3. Notified Area Committee
● Set up for:
○ Rapidly growing towns (often due to industrialization).
○ Emerging towns that don’t yet meet criteria for becoming municipalities.
● Created by notification in the state gazette.
● Functions under relevant provisions of the State Municipal Act, only to the extent specified in the
notification.
● May also derive powers from other acts.
● All members including the chairman are nominated by the state government.
● Hence, it is neither a statutory body nor an elected body, but performs functions similar to a
municipality.
4. Town Area Committee
● Created for the administration of small towns.
● Considered a semi-municipal authority.
● Responsible for limited civic duties such as:
○ Drainage
○ Road maintenance
○ Street lighting
○ Sanitation/conservancy
● Established through a separate act of the state legislature.
● The committee may be:
○ Entirely elected
○ Entirely nominated
○ Or a combination of both
Note: The A.P. Jain Committee (1963–66) recommended merging small town area committees
with Panchayati Raj institutions to avoid administrative duplication.
5. Cantonment Board
● Administers civilian populations within military cantonment areas.
● Established under the Cantonments Act, 2006 (replacing the 1924 Act).
● Functions under the Ministry of Defence.
● Created and governed directly by the Central Government, unlike municipalities which are under
state control.
Purpose of the 2006 Act:
● Democratization of cantonment governance.
● Strengthening of financial base.
● Promotion of development within cantonments.
Classification of Cantonment Boards:
Category Civil Population
I Above 50,000
II 10,000 to 50,000
III 2,500 to 10,000
IV Below 2,500
Currently, 62 Cantonment Boards exist in India.
A Cantonment Area refers to a legally defined zone where troops are permanently stationed.
● A Cantonment Board includes both elected and nominated (ex-officio) members.
● Elected members serve for five years.
● Nominated members (ex-officio) continue as long as they hold their official positions.
● The station commander (military officer) is the ex-officio president and chairs the meetings.
● The Vice-President is elected from among the elected members and holds office for five years.
Composition of Category I Cantonment Board:
1. Station Commander (Military Officer) – Ex-officio President
2. Executive Engineer
3. Health Officer
4. First Class Magistrate (nominated by District Magistrate)
5. Three Military Officers (nominated by station commander)
6. Eight elected members (from cantonment residents)
7. Chief Executive Officer (appointed by the President of India)
Key Points:
● Functions of the board are similar to a municipality:
○ Divided into obligatory and discretionary functions.
● Income sources include tax and non-tax revenue.
● Executive Officer:
○ Appointed by the President of India.
○ Belongs to a central cadre.
○ Implements resolutions and decisions of the board.
7. Township
● Established by large public enterprises to provide civic amenities to their employees living in
nearby housing colonies.
● Entirely administrative in structure, without elected representatives.
● The enterprise appoints a Town Administrator, assisted by engineers and technical staff.
● It is considered an extension of the enterprise’s bureaucracy, not a local self-government body.
8. Port Trust
● Created to manage major port cities like Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, etc.
● Set up by Acts of Parliament.
● Functions:
○ Manage and safeguard port infrastructure.
○ Provide civic amenities similar to a municipality.
● Composition:
○ Includes elected and nominated members.
○ Chaired by a government-appointed official.
9. Special Purpose Agency
● These are function-based, not area-based like municipalities.
● Set up by states to carry out specific civic functions that usually fall under municipal authorities.
● Also called:
○ Single-purpose, uni-purpose, or functional local bodies.
● Created either by:
○ State legislative acts (as statutory bodies)
○ Executive resolutions (as departments)
Examples of Special Purpose Agencies:
● Town Improvement Trusts
● Urban Development Authorities
● Water Supply and Sewerage Boards
● Housing Boards
● Pollution Control Boards
● Electricity Supply Boards
● City Transport Boards
These agencies function independently from municipalities and are not subordinate to them.
MUNICIPAL PERSONNEL SYSTEM IN INDIA
Urban local bodies in India follow three types of municipal personnel systems:
1. Separate Personnel System
● Each local body independently appoints, controls, and manages its own staff.
● Personnel are not transferable to other local bodies.
● Most commonly followed system in India.
● Promotes local autonomy and loyalty to a single institution.
2. Unified Personnel System
● The state government appoints, controls, and administers the municipal personnel.
● A state-wide cadre is created.
● Officers are transferable between urban local bodies within the state.
● Prevalent in: Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh etc.
3. Integrated Personnel System
● Personnel of local bodies are part of the state civil services.
● Transferable between local bodies and state government departments.
● No distinction between local and state civil service.
● Prevalent in: Odisha, Bihar, Karnataka, Punjab, Haryana etc.
MUNICIPAL REVENUE SOURCES
1. Tax Revenue
● Property tax (most significant)
● Entertainment tax, advertisement tax, professional tax
● Taxes on animals, water, lighting, pilgrims, markets, tolls
● Cesses: Library cess, education cess, beggary cess
2. Non-Tax Revenue
● Rent from municipal property
● Fees, fines, royalties, dividends, interest
● User charges: water, sanitation, sewerage, etc.
3. Grants
● From Central and State Governments
● Used for infrastructure, development schemes, and reforms
4. Devolution
● Funds transferred by State Government to urban local bodies
● Based on recommendations of State Finance Commission
5. Loans
● Raised from state government or financial institutions
● Require state government approval for borrowing
CENTRAL COUNCIL OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT
● Established in 1954 under Article 263 of the Constitution.
● Initially named Central Council of Local Self-Government, renamed in the 1980s.
● Since 1958, it deals only with urban local governance.
Composition:
● Union Minister for Housing and Urban Affairs – Chairman
● State Ministers responsible for local self-government
Functions:
1. Recommends policy matters on urban governance
2. Proposes legislations
3. Facilitates Centre–State cooperation
4. Develops a common action programme
5. Recommends financial assistance from the Centre
6. Reviews the functioning of local bodies receiving Central funds
