The following is the complete text extracted from the source **"Fundamental duties (1).pdf"**, organized by its original sections and thematic presentation:

### **I. Origins and Introduction**
*   **Location:** Enumerated in **Part IV A**, **Article 51 A** of the Constitution.
*   **Recommendation:** Added based on the **Swaran Singh Committee (1976)** recommendations.
*   **Quantity:** The committee recommended 8 duties, but the **42nd Amendment (1976)** included 10.
*   **Borrowing:** These duties were borrowed from the **erstwhile USSR**.
*   **Expansion:** An **11th Fundamental Duty** was added in **2002** through the **86th Amendment**.

### **II. Recommendations Not Accepted**
The Congress Party did not incorporate certain committee recommendations, including:
1.  Parliament providing penalties or punishment for non-compliance.
2.  Prohibiting courts from questioning such penalty laws on the grounds of infringing Fundamental Rights.
3.  Making the **duty to pay taxes** a Fundamental Duty.

### **III. Relationship Between Rights and Duties**
*   **Correlative Nature:** Rights and duties go hand-in-hand; the right of one person may be the duty of another.
*   **Enforceability:** Rights are enforceable only when others fulfill their duties.
*   **Object:** To remind citizens that they must be conscious of their duties while enforcing their rights.
*   **Democratic Balance:** Indira Gandhi noted that duties establish a "democratic balance" rather than smothering rights.

### **IV. Classification of Duties**
Duties are categorized as follows:
*   **Primary and Secondary:** Primary duties are independent; secondary (remedial) duties depend on others.
*   **Positive and Negative:** Positive duties are prescribed by law; negative duties are prohibited by law.
*   **Absolute and Relative (Austin’s Classification):** Relative duties relate to specific rights, while absolute duties do not.
*   **Moral vs. Civic:** Cherishing freedom ideals is a **moral precept**, while respecting the National Flag is a **civic duty**.

### **V. The 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**
It shall be the duty of every citizen of India:
*   **a)** To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, institutions, the National Flag, and National Anthem.
*   **b)** To cherish and follow noble ideals from the national struggle for freedom.
*   **c)** To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
*   **d)** To defend the country and render national service when called upon.
*   **e)** To promote harmony and common brotherhood across diversities and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
*   **f)** To value and preserve the rich heritage of composite culture.
*   **g)** To protect and improve the natural environment (forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife) and have compassion for living creatures.
*   **h)** To develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
*   **i)** To safeguard public property and abjure violence.
*   **j)** To strive toward excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.
*   **k)** For a parent or guardian to provide educational opportunities to their child/ward aged **6 to 14 years** (added by 86th CAA, 2002).

### **VI. Features and Scope**
*   **Confined to Citizens:** Unlike some Fundamental Rights that extend to foreigners, all Fundamental Duties are **confined to citizens only**.
*   **Non-Justiciable:** They are not directly enforceable by courts, and there are no legal sanctions for their violation in the Constitution itself.
*   **Legislative Enforcement:** Parliament is free to enforce them through suitable legislation.

### **VII. Criticism and Significance**
*   **Criticisms:**
    *   **Incomplete:** Excludes important duties like voting or paying taxes.
    *   **Vague:** Uses complex or ambiguous terms like "composite culture".
    *   **Placement:** Placed after DPSPs (Part IV-A) rather than after Fundamental Rights (Part III), which critics say reduces their importance.
*   **Significance:**
    *   Serves as a warning against **anti-national activities** (e.g., burning the flag, destroying property).
    *   Acts as a source of inspiration and promotes discipline.
    *   **Judicial Aid:** Helps courts determine the "reasonableness" of a law under **Article 14 or 19** if the law seeks to give effect to a fundamental duty.

### **VIII. Justice Verma Recommendations**
The National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution recommended:
*   Sensitizing the public and creating general awareness.
*   Guarding the freedom of religion and minority rights.
*   **Education Reform:** Using education to inculcate the willpower to adhere to duties.
*   Including the **duty to vote** and **pay taxes** in Article 51A.

### **IX. Existing Legal Provisions for Enforcement**
Several laws currently enforce these duties:
1.  **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971):** Respect for Flag and Anthem.
2.  **Criminal Laws:** Punish enmity on grounds of religion, race, etc.
3.  **Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955):** Caste and religion-related offences.
4.  **Indian Penal Code (IPC):** Punishes assertions prejudicial to national integration.
5.  **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (1967):** Communal organisations.
6.  **Representation of People Act (1951):** Disqualification for corrupt practices (e.g., soliciting votes on religion).
7.  **Wildlife (Protection) Act (1972):** Protection of endangered species.
8.  **Forest (Conservation) Act (1980):** Checks deforestation.

The following is a comprehensive extraction of all text from the source **"Fundamental Duties.pdf"**, organized by section and topic as presented in the document:

### **I. Introduction and Background**
*   **Enumeration:** Found in **Part IVA, Article 51A** of the Constitution.
*   **Original Constitution (1950):** Contained only Fundamental Rights, not Fundamental Duties.
*   **Philosophy:** Rights and duties are correlative and inseparable.
*   **Initial Exclusion:** Framers did not feel it necessary to include them for citizens, though they included duties of the State in the form of **DPSPs**.
*   **Historical Context:** 
    *   **1976:** Formally added via the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act**.
    *   **2002:** An 11th duty (education for children) was added via the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act**.
*   **Inspiration:** Inspired by the **erstwhile USSR (Soviet Union)**, which gave equal importance to both rights and duties.
*   **Democratic Comparison:** Major democratic constitutions (USA, Canada, France, etc.) do not list citizen duties; the **Japanese Constitution** is a rare exception that does.

### **II. Swaran Singh Committee Recommendations (1976)**
*   **Origin:** Set up during the **Internal Emergency (1975–1977)** to recommend changes.
*   **Core Emphasis:** Citizens must realize that enjoying rights comes with obligations to the nation.
*   **Outcome:** Acceptance led to the insertion of **Part IVA** and a code of ten duties.
*   **Unaccepted Recommendations:**
    1.  Empowering Parliament to prescribe **penalties or punishment** for non-compliance.
    2.  **Exclusion from judicial review** for laws imposing such penalties.
    3.  Making the **duty to pay taxes** a Fundamental Duty.

### **III. The 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**
It is the duty of every citizen of India to:
1.  **Respect National Symbols:** Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, institutions, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
2.  **Uphold Freedom Struggle Ideals:** Cherish and follow noble ideals from the national struggle for independence.
3.  **Protect Sovereignty:** Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
4.  **Defend the Nation:** Render national service when called upon.
5.  **Promote Harmony:** Encourage brotherhood transcending religious, linguistic, and regional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the **dignity of women**.
6.  **Preserve Culture:** Value and preserve the rich heritage of India’s composite culture.
7.  **Environment & Compassion:** Protect natural environments (forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife) and show compassion for living creatures.
8.  **Scientific Outlook:** Develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry/reform.
9.  **Safeguard Public Property:** Protect public property and abjure violence.
10. **Strive for Excellence:** Strive for excellence in all individual and collective spheres to rise to higher levels of achievement.
11. **Ensure Education:** Parents/guardians must provide educational opportunities to children aged **6 to 14 years** (Added in 2002).

### **IV. Features of Fundamental Duties**
*   **Nature:** Categorized as **moral** (cherishing ideals) and **civic** (respecting the Flag/Anthem) obligations.
*   **Tradition:** They are a codification of tasks integral to the **Indian way of life**, rooted in tradition, culture, and mythology.
*   **Scope:** Restricted **only to citizens**; unlike some FRs, they do not apply to foreigners.
*   **Non-Justiciable:** Not legally enforceable by courts; no direct constitutional sanction for violation.
*   **Legislative Enforcement:** Parliament is free to enforce them through specific legislation.

### **V. Criticisms**
*   **Not Exhaustive:** Leaves out duties like casting votes, paying taxes, and family planning.
*   **Vague/Ambiguous:** Terms like "noble ideals," "composite culture," and "scientific temper" are difficult for common citizens to interpret.
*   **Moral Precepts:** Critics argue that without enforceability, they are merely a code of conduct.
*   **Superfluous:** Many duties (like respecting the flag) would be followed even without constitutional mention.
*   **Placement:** Placement as an appendage to Part IV (DPSPs) is seen as reducing their significance compared to Fundamental Rights.

### **VI. Significance**
*   **Responsibility:** Reminds citizens of their duties to society and fellow citizens.
*   **Warning:** Acts as a safeguard against anti-national acts like flag burning or destroying public property.
*   **Inspiration:** Promotes discipline and active participation in nation-building.
*   **Judicial Aid:** Assists courts in determining the **constitutional validity of laws** (e.g., *Mohan Kumar Singhania Case* (1991) and *Ramlila Maidan Incident Case* (2012)).

### **VII. Political Perspectives**
*   **H. R. Gokhale (Law Minister):** Noted they would have a "sobering effect" on restless and anti-national spirits.
*   **Indira Gandhi:** Argued they establish a "democratic balance" by making people conscious of duties alongside rights.
*   **Political Consensus:** Though initially opposed by the Opposition, the **Janata Government (Morarji Desai)** did not annul them, showing eventual cross-party agreement on their necessity.

### **VIII. Verma Committee Findings (1999)**
The committee identified existing legal provisions that implement these duties:
*   **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971):** Respect for Flag, Anthem, and Constitution.
*   **Criminal Laws:** Punish enmity on grounds of race, language, or religion.
*   **Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955):** Caste and religion-related offences.
*   **Indian Penal Code (IPC):** Punishes assertions prejudicial to national integration.
*   **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967:** Declaration of communal organisations as unlawful.
*   **Representation of the People Act (1951):** Disqualifies representatives for corrupt practices or soliciting votes on religious grounds.
*   **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972:** Safeguards biodiversity.
*   **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980:** Checks indiscriminate deforestation.
