**Topic:** Appointment of Judges

---

### Key Judicial Appointment Cases Timeline

#### 1. First Judges Case: *S.P. Gupta vs. Union of India (1981)*

* **Principle:** Judicial Independence is an integral part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution. Neither Parliament nor the Executive can restrain Judicial Independence.
* **Ruling:** Consultation with the Chief Justice of India (CJI) in judicial appointments is **NOT binding**, but mere consultation.
* **Process Flow:** `President` $\rightarrow$ Consults with `Chief Justice of concerned High Court` $\rightarrow$ Recommends names $\rightarrow$ President decides.

#### 2. Summary List of Judges Cases:

1. **First Judges Case (1981):** Consultation $\neq$ Concurrence (Not binding).
2. **Second Judges Case (1993):** Consultation $=$ Concurrence (Binding).
3. **Third Judges Case (1998):** Collegium System established ($4+1$ for SC; $2+1$ for HC).
4. **Fourth Judges Case (2014):** NJAC declared unconstitutional.

#### 3. Second Judges Case: *Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association vs. Union of India (1993)*

* Supreme Court reaffirmed the independence clause under the Basic Structure.
* Reversed its 1981 decision: Consultation with the CJI in judicial appointments is treated as **BINDING**.
* The President cannot refuse what the CJI recommends.

#### 4. Third Judges Case (1998) — Current System

* Established the **Collegium System** (a body of senior judges appointing other judges).

---

### Judges Inquiry Act, 1968 (Detailed Removal Process)

1. **Initiation:** An MP from either House (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha) can move a removal motion.
2. **Signature Support Required:**
* **Lok Sabha:** At least **100 MPs**
* **Rajya Sabha:** At least **50 MPs**


3. **Admission:** The Presiding Officer (Speaker/Chairman) decides whether to admit or reject the motion. If admitted, a 3-member committee is set up.
4. **3-Member Inquiry Committee Composition:**
* CJI or a Senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court
* Chief Justice of a High Court
* A Distinguished Jurist


5. **Investigation:** The Committee investigates the charges and presents its report to Parliament.
6. **If Found Guilty:**
* Parliament votes on the report.
* Decision of Committee is **NOT BINDING** on Parliament/President to pass the motion.
* Requires **Special Majority** in both Houses: $\frac{1}{2}\text{ of Total Membership} + \frac{2}{3}\text{ Present and Voting}$.


7. **If Found NOT Guilty:**
* The decision is **FINAL & BINDING**.
* Proceedings end immediately; no further impeachment can occur.

## PAGE 4

**Topic:** Powers of Supreme Court & High Court

---

### 1. Original Jurisdiction

* **Definition:** Power of a court to hear a case in the first instance (directly).

#### Supreme Court Exclusive Original Jurisdiction (Article 131 - Set Diagram representation)

* Explicitly defined in the Constitution (cannot be altered by ordinary acts):
* $\text{Center (C)} \text{ vs. } \text{State (S)}$
* $\text{State}_1 \text{ vs. } \text{State}_2$
* $(\text{State}_1, \text{State}_2) \text{ vs. } (\text{State}_3, \text{State}_4)$
* $\text{State}_1 \text{ vs. } \text{Center (C)} + \text{State}_2$



#### High Court Original Jurisdiction

* Disputes regarding Election of MPs / MLAs (e.g., *Indira Gandhi vs. Raj Narain* case).
* Revenue matters & Legal Rights enforcement.

---

### 2. Appellate Jurisdiction

* **Definition:** Power to hear appeals against lower court judgments.
* **Branches:** Civil Cases & Criminal Cases.
* **Special Leave Petition (SLP) - Article 136:**
* Exclusive discretionary power of the Supreme Court.
* SC can grant special leave to appeal against any judgment/order from any court/tribunal in India.
* Landmark Case: *Arnesh Kumar vs. State of Bihar*.



---

### 3. Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143)

* President seeks opinion/advice from the Supreme Court.
* **General Law / Public Policy Matter:** Supreme Court **may refuse** to provide advice (e.g., Same-sex marriage reference).
* **Pre-Constitutional Treaties/Agreements (Prior to 1950):** Supreme Court **CANNOT refuse** to give advice.

---

### 4. Writ Jurisdiction

| Supreme Court (Article 32) | High Court (Article 226) |
| --- | --- |
| Direct approach allowed under Fundamental Rights. | Direct approach allowed for rights enforcement. |
| **5 Types of Writs:** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto. | **5 Types of Writs:** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto. |
| **Narrower Scope:** Restricted **ONLY** to Fundamental Rights violations. | **Wider Scope:** Covers Fundamental Rights **AND** Any Other Legal Right violations. |

## PAGE 5

**Topic:** Judicial Review, Judicial Activism & Judicial Restraint

---

### Judicial Review

* **Definition:** Power of SC and HC to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders (both Central and State).
* If found **Ultra Vires** (beyond constitutional powers), Judiciary declares them illegal, unconstitutional, and invalid.
* **Key Supporting Articles:** Article 13, Article 32, Articles 131–136, Article 143, Article 226, Article 246.

---

```
                             │

```

* **Procedure Established by Law:** Law is examined **only on procedural grounds** (whether proper legislative procedure was followed). Reasonableness is not judged.
* **Due Process of Law:** Law is examined on **both procedural AND substantive grounds** (if arbitrary, unfair, or unreasonable, it is declared invalid even if properly passed).

#### Key Concepts of Judicial Activism:

* **Pioneers in India:** Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer and Justice P.N. Bhagwati.
* **Locus Standi Relaxation:**
* *Traditional Locus Standi:* Only the aggrieved party can approach the court.
* *Relaxed Rule:* Any public-spirited citizen or social organization can approach the court on behalf of affected parties.


* **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** Result of relaxed *Locus Standi*. Also termed as:
* **SAL** (Social Action Litigation)
* **SIL** (Social Interest Litigation)
* **CAL** (Class Action Litigation)

## PAGE 6

**Topic:** Tribunals

---

### Background & Origin

* **Not part of original Constitution.**
* Added via **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** based on the recommendations of the **Swaran Singh Committee**.
* **Purpose:** Specialized justice delivery, involving technical experts, and reducing workload on conventional courts.

---

### Articles Added for Tribunals

* **Article 323A:** Administrative Tribunals ONLY (e.g., CAT – Central Administrative Tribunal, SAT – State Administrative Tribunal).
* **Article 323B:** Tribunals for other matters (e.g., ITAT – Income Tax, NCLAT, Armed Forces Tribunal, NGT).

---

### Chronological Evolution & Case Law Timeline

```
1969 ──► First Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) recommends Tribunals
  │
1974 ──► Law Commission of India Report on Tribunals
  │
1976 ──► 42nd Amendment Act (Articles 323A & 323B added)
  │
1980 ──► Expansion of Tribunal System
  │
1986 ──► S.P. Sampath Kumar Case
         • SC held Parliament can create Tribunals parallel to High Courts.
         • Tribunals shared equal power with HCs; not subordinate to HCs.
         • Appointment of Judicial/Executive members requires CJI / CJ(HC) consultation.
  │
1997 ──► L. Chandra Kumar Case
         • SC overruled S.P. Sampath case.
         • Declared Tribunals SUBORDINATE to High Courts.
         • All decisions subject to HC Judicial Review under Art 226/227.
         • Judicial members preferred for heading Tribunals.
  │
2010 ──► R. Gandhi Case vs. Union of India
         • SC held Judicial members must have primary power.
         • Technical members must be at least Secretary-level officers.
  │
2017 ──► Finance Act, 2017
         • Merged several Tribunals.
         • Central Govt assumed rule-making power for appointments, tenure, and removal.
  │
2019 ──► Rojer Mathew Case
         • SC held judicial functions of Tribunals must be performed by Judicial members.
         • Central Govt cannot arbitrarily dictate tenure or removal.
  │
2021 ──► Tribunal Reforms Act, 2021
         • Fixed minimum age for Chairperson/Members at 50 years.
         • Fixed tenure to 4 years (Max age: 70 for Chairperson, 67 for Members).
  │
2021 ──► Madras Bar Association Case (2021)
         • SC upheld the Act in general, but STRUCK DOWN:
           × Minimum age criteria of 50 years
           × 4-year tenure provision

```
