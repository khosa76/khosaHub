## **Page 2: Election of the President**

1. **What is the type of election?**
* **Ans:** Indirect Election.




2. **Who participates in the Indirect Election (Electoral College)?**
* **Ans:** Only elected personnel:
* a) MPs (Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha)


* b) MLAs (State Legislative Assemblies)






3. **Can Nominated MPs/MLAs vote?**
* **Ans:** No.




4. **Which Law/Act governs the election?**
* **Ans:** President and Vice-President Elections Act, 1952.




5. **What is the system of Election?**
* **Ans:** Proportional Representation by Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV) along with the value attached to each vote.




6. **Does the election require a majority of votes?**
* **Ans:** Yes, the total value of votes secured must be **more than 50%** of the total vote value.




7. **Is there any difference between the value of the vote of an MP and an MLA?**
* Each MP's vote value is greater than that of an individual MLA.


* The **Total Value of votes of all MPs** is equal to the **Total Value of votes of all MLAs**.




8. **Formulas to Determine Vote Values:**
* **Value of Vote of an MLA:**

$$\text{Value of MLA Vote} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Population of State}}{\text{Total Elected MLAs}} \right) \times \frac{1}{1000}$$



* **Value of Vote of an MP:**

$$\text{Value of MP Vote} = \frac{\text{Total Value of Votes of All MLAs of All States}}{\text{Total Elected MPs}}$$

4. **MLAs / State Legislative Assemblies** do NOT participate.

6. **No Joint Session** is allowed in case of a deadlock.

## **Page 4: Ordinance Making Power**

* **Article 123** empowers the President to promulgate ordinances during the recess of Parliament.



### **Key Characteristics:**

* **Not a Discretionary Power:** The President can promulgate an ordinance **only on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister**.


* **Not a Parallel Power of Legislation:** The government cannot bypass Parliament or compel the President to pass an ordinance simply because they are unable to get a bill passed in Parliament.


* **Retrospective Effect:** An ordinance can be retrospective in effect.


* **Inapplicable Scope:** This power is **not applicable to Constitutional Amendments**.



### **Constitutional Safeguards:**

* The President can promulgate an ordinance **ONLY when BOTH Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha are not in session**, OR when **either of the Houses is not in session**.
(Even if Rajya Sabha is in session and Lok Sabha is not, an ordinance can be issued.)


* An ordinance can be issued only on subjects on which Parliament has the power to make laws (**7th Schedule: Union List, Concurrent List, and Residuary subjects**).


* Every ordinance **must be laid before Parliament within 6 weeks** from the reassembly of Parliament.



### **Important Constitutional Amendments:**

* **38th Amendment Act:** Added a provision stating that the President's satisfaction regarding issuing an ordinance is final and beyond Judicial Review.


* **44th Constitutional Amendment Act (1978):** Deleted the above provision. As per the 44th Amendment, the satisfaction of the President **can be challenged in court**.

## **Page 5: Pardoning Power of the President**

* **Article 72** empowers the President to grant pardons to persons convicted of offenses in all cases where:
1. Punishment or sentence is for an offense against a **Central/Union Law** (e.g., conviction under BNS/IPC).


2. Punishment or sentence is by a **Court Martial (Military Court)**.


3. The sentence is a **Death Sentence (Capital Punishment)**.




* **Independence:** This power of the President is independent of the Judiciary.


* **Nature:** The President does not sit as a Court of Appeal.



### **Types of Pardoning Powers:**

1. **Full Pardon:** Absolves the convict completely from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications (removes both conviction and sentence).


2. **Commutation:** Substitution of one form of punishment for a **lighter form**.


* *Example:* A death sentence commuted to life imprisonment.




3. **Remission:** Reducing the **quantum/duration** of sentence without changing its character/nature.


* *Example:* Rigorous imprisonment for 7 years remitted to rigorous imprisonment for 3 years.




4. **Respite:** Awarding a **lesser sentence** in place of the one originally awarded due to special facts.


* *Example:* Reducing 5 years of rigorous imprisonment to 3 years due to the pregnancy of a female offender.




5. **Reprieve:** Temporary **stay of execution** of a sentence for a temporary period.
