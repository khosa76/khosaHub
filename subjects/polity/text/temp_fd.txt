9. Fundamental Duties
Aspect
	Details
	Incorporation
	Added by the 42nd CAA, 1976 on Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation.
	Placement
	Part IV-A, Article 51A (Initially 10 duties, one more added in 86th Amendment, 2002). 
	Inspiration
	Inspired by the USSR; Japan is the only country with a constitutional list of duties.
	Fundamental Duties
	1. Respect the Constitution, national flag, anthem. 
2. Follow noble ideals of the freedom struggle. 
3. Uphold & protect sovereignty, unity, integrity (preamble). 
4. Defend the country, serve when called upon. 
5. Promote harmony, fraternity (preamble), renounce practices derogatory to women. 
6. Preserve composite cultural heritage. 
7. Protect the environment, forests, wildlife, show compassion to living creatures (DPSP).
8. Develop scientific temper, humanism, and reform spirit. 
9. Safeguard public property, avoid violence. 
10. Strive for excellence in all spheres. 
11. Provide educational opportunities (Added by 86th Amendment, 2002).
	Nature of Duties
	- Moral (e.g., cherishing noble ideals). 
- Civic (e.g., respecting the Constitution).
	Legal Status
	- Not legally enforceable (non-justiciable). 
- No penalties for violation unless provided by law.
	Criticism
	- Excludes Justice verma committee recommendation (e.g., voting, paying taxes, help) -some recommendations of swaran singh committee (total 8 recommendations), but some not included - ex - penalty for not following duties, paying taxes)  
- Some are vague (e.g., "noble ideals").
- No legal enforcement, making them a mere code of conduct. 
	Significance
	-   Rights & Duties are correlative- Reminds citizens of their responsibilities. 
- Discourages anti-national & anti-social activities. 
- Supreme Court (1992): A law upholding FD is considered reasonable. 
- Parliament ONLY can impose penalties for violations.
	Verma Committee Observations
	-identify Existing laws to help implement duties: 
1. Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act (1971) – Protects national symbols.
                     * First Offence: Punishable by up to three years imprisonment, fines, or both.
                     * Subsequent Offences: Involves mandatory minimum imprisonment of one year.
2. Criminal Laws – Punish acts inciting enmity. 
3. Protection of Civil Rights Act (1955) – Penalizes caste-based offences.
