## Article 23 & Article 24 (Right against Exploitation)

### Article 23: Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour

* **3 Evils Addressed**:


1. Trafficking


2. Begar


3. Forced Labour




* **Key Provisions**:


1. Prohibits trafficking of human beings along with forced labour.


2. If any person is caught pursuing human trafficking and forced labour, they are prosecuted under appropriate laws.


3. Parliament has drafted laws in accordance with this Article:


* **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956**: To stop human trafficking (e.g., sex trafficking, organ trafficking, child trafficking).


* **Minimum Wages Act / Equal Remuneration Act**: For controlling forced labour and ensuring fair remuneration/wages.






* **Exceptions under Clause (2) of Article 23**:


* The State can impose compulsory service, such as conscription (compulsory recruitment of citizens for the military).


* Such compulsory service shall be free from discrimination (under Article 15).





---

### Article 24: Prohibition of Employment of Children in Factories, etc.

* **Key Provision**: Prohibits child labour. No child from age 0 to 14 shall be employed to work in any factory, mine, or any hazardous employment.


* **Acts Created by Parliament**:


1. **Child & Adolescent Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act, 1986** (amended in 2016).


2. **Factories Act, 1948**.




* **Exceptions Provided under Statutory Acts**:


* Work in a family enterprise, or as an artist (films, TV, advertisements, sports).


* *Conditions*: Work must not be in circuses, must take place after school hours or during vacations, must not be hazardous, and other legal safeguards must be satisfied.




* **Statutory Body**:


* **National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR)**: Recommends policy and focuses on child development in India.

## Motions in Parliament

### Diagram: Motion Procedure Flowchart

```
[ Any MP of either house will keep a proposal ] 
                       ↓
[ Formal proposal to start a discussion in Parliament / State Legislature ] 
                       ↓
[ Presiding Officer (Speaker / Chairperson) ] 
                       ↓
[ Approve ]
```[cite: 1]

### Definition
* A **Motion** is a formal proposal by an MP/MLA to start a discussion on the floor of the House[cite: 1].

### Step-by-Step Procedure
1. **Step 1: Proposals** – Any MP/MLA can propose a motion to the presiding officer of the House[cite: 1].
2. **Step 2: Approval** – The presiding officer approves the motion and puts it out for an open discussion[cite: 1].
3. **Step 3: Debate** – The full House discusses the motion[cite: 1].
4. **Step 4: Voting** – After sufficient discussion, the motion is put to a vote[cite: 1]. It passes when the House fetches "yes" votes by a simple majority (half of the members present and voting)[cite: 1].

#### 1. Procedural Motion
* Focuses on starting a generic debate on any topic in Parliament[cite: 1].
* Does not consist of the removal of the government or a minister[cite: 1].

#### 2. Restrictive Motions
* **1) Adjournment Motion**[cite: 1]:
  * Introduced in Parliament to draw attention to a matter of urgent public importance[cite: 1].
  * Changes the set agenda of the House to an urgent topic[cite: 1].
  * Requires the support of 50 members to be admitted/approved by the Presiding Officer to speak[cite: 1].
* **2) Censure Motion**[cite: 1]:
  * Purpose is to restrict/censure a minister on the floor of the House[cite: 1].
  * Must mandatorily state the reasons for its adoption[cite: 1].
  * Can be moved against an individual minister, a group of ministers, or the Council of Ministers[cite: 1].
  * The government does not need to resign from office if it is passed in the Lok Sabha[cite: 1].
* **3) No-Confidence Motion (Article 75)**[cite: 1]:
  * Article 75 states that the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha[cite: 1].
  * The ministry stays in office as long as it enjoys the confidence of the majority in the Lok Sabha[cite: 1].
  * The Lok Sabha can remove the entire Council of Ministers by passing a No-Confidence Motion[cite: 1].
  * No reasons are required to adopt this motion[cite: 1].
  * Needs the support of 50 members to be admitted[cite: 1].
  * Always moved against the entire Council of Ministers, not against individual ministers[cite: 1].

#### 3. Special Motion
* **Motion of Thanks**[cite: 1]:
  * **Occasions**:
    a) Towards the start of a new Lok Sabha[cite: 1].
    b) At the start of the Budget Session / financial year / new session of the year[cite: 1].
  * The President can address the House anytime if needed (though an unscheduled address has never happened in the history of independent India)[cite: 1].
  * **Major Condition**: It must pass in both Houses; the Council of Ministers must resign if it fails to pass in the Lok Sabha[cite: 1].

## Parliamentary Privileges (Immunities)
*(Article 105[2], Article 194[2])*[cite: 1]

Under these articles, certain rights and immunities are provided to MPs/MLAs, known as Parliamentary Privileges[cite: 1].

### Key Categories & Features:

1. **Individual Privileges**[cite: 1]:
   * Rights & immunities enjoyed by MPs/MLAs as individuals[cite: 1].
   * *Example*: Freedom of speech and expression for anything said/expressed on the floor of the House[cite: 1].
   * No prosecution will take place for anything said in Parliament or a State Legislative Assembly[cite: 1].

2. **Institutional Privileges**[cite: 1]:
   * Privileges enjoyed by Parliament / State Legislative Assemblies as an institution[cite: 1].
   * *Examples*:
     * a) No MP/MLA can be arrested during a session of the House[cite: 1].
     * b) No arrest can be made on the premises of the legislature (building of Parliament / State Legislative Assembly)[cite: 1].
     * c) Immunity from arrest applies 40 days prior to and 40 days after a session ($\pm 40\text{ days}$)[cite: 1].
     * d) An arrest can only be made by leave of the House (permission by the Presiding Officer)[cite: 1].

3. **Major Supreme Court Decisions**[cite: 1]:
   1. **Destruction of Property inside the House**: Is **NOT** considered a breach of privilege[cite: 1].
   2. **Scope of Privileges**: The Supreme Court held that these privileges are provided to MPs/MLAs solely to perform legislative functions without hindrance or delay[cite: 1]. These privileges are **not** gateways to claim exemption from criminal law[cite: 1].
