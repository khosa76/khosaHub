6. Citizenship
Aspect
	Details
	Meaning & Significance
	Citizenship grants full rights and privileges. Aliens (friendly/enemy) have restricted rights. Enemy aliens can be detained/arrested without protection.
	Rights Exclusive to Citizens
	- Fundamental Rights: Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30. 
- Political Rights: Right to vote, contest elections (Parliament), hold public offices (e.g., President, Governor, PM, etc.). 
- Eligibility for President: Naturalized + Born citizens in India eligible. In the USA, only birth citizens can become President.
	Constitutional Provisions
	- Articles 5-11 (Part 2): Define citizenship provisions. 
- Parliament's Authority: Empowered to regulate citizenship through laws.
	Key Citizenship Articles
	- Article 5-8: Defines four categories of citizens at the commencement of the Constitution (26 Jan 1950). 
- Article 9: Indian citizenship lost if a person voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship. 
- Article 10: Citizenship is subject to laws made by Parliament. 
- Article 11: Parlia has the power to regulate acquisition, termination, and other citizenship matters.
	Citizenship Act, 1955
	- Governs citizenship acquisition, termination, and supplementary provisions. 
- Amended 6 times (originally included Commonwealth citizenship, repealed in 2003). 
- ‘Domicile’ is an important criterion mentioned in consti  for citizenship but is not defined in the Constitution.
- 5 ways to get citizenship; 3 ways to lose citizenship
            * Key Amendments to the Citizenship Act, 1955 (6 so far)
            * (i) CAA, 1986- The original 1955 Act gave citizenship to anyone born in India. The 1986 change ensured that children born to foreigners in India did not automatically get citizenship unless one parent was already an Indian citizen.
            * (ii)1992
            * (iii) CAA 2003= Introduced the concept of OCI.
            * (iv) CAA, 2005- nothing imp
            * (v) CAA, 2015-  Merged PIO and OCI schemes.
            * (vi) CAA 2019
            * Provided a fast-track citizenship process (naturalization in 5 years instead of 11) for persecuted religious minorities (Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, and Christians) from Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Afghanistan who entered India before Dec 31, 2014.
            * Excluded Muslims from these 3 countries from the benefit.
            * Exempted such migrants from being classified as illegal immigrants.
            * Not applicable to tribal areas of the Northeast and certain regions under ILP (Inner Line Permit- Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal and Mizoram- NagMani ArMi ).
	Acquisition of Citizenship


In India, citizenship is granted by the Ministry of Home Affairs (Central Government) according to the rules and laws regulated by Parliament
	1. By Birth: Automatic
- Before 1 July 1987: Citizen irrespective of parents' nationality. 
- 1 July 1987 – 2 Dec 2004: Either parent must be an Indian citizen at birth time. 
- After 3 Dec 2004: Both parents must be Indian citizens or one Indian + the other not an illegal immigrant. 
- Exceptions: Enemy aliens and children of foreign diplomats in India are not granted citizenship by birth. 
2. By Descent: 
- If born outside India on/after 3 Dec 2004, not a citizen unless registered at an Indian consulate within one year. 
- Requires permission from the Central Government after one year. 
3. By Registration: (Eligible categories) 
- Indian Origin Resident: Lived in India for 7 years before applying. 
- Indian Origin Abroad: Residing outside undivided India. 
- Spouse of Indian Citizen: Married + resident in India for 7 years before applying. 
- Minor Child: Born to Indian citizens. 
- Person with Indian Parent(s): Either parent was an Indian citizen at independence; must reside in India for 12 months before applying. 
4. By Naturalization: 
- Applicants must renounce foreign citizenship and not belong to a country that prohibits Indian naturalization. 
- Must reside in India or serve in its government for 12 months before applying. 
- In the preceding 12 years, you must have lived in India for at least 11 years. 
- Post-naturalization, must reside in India or serve in an Indian-recognized intnl org. 
5. By Incorporation of Territory: 
- When a foreign territory is added to India, its residents become Indian citizens (e.g., Pondicherry, 1962).
	Termination of Citizenship
	1. By Renunciation: 
- If an Indian citizen voluntarily renounces citizenship, their minor child also loses citizenship unless one parent remains Indian. 
- A child who loses citizenship can regain it after attaining legal adulthood. 
2. By Termination: 
- If an Indian acquires foreign citizenship voluntarily, Indian citizenship is automatically terminated. 
- Exception: Does not apply during war. 
3. By Deprivation (Government action): 
- Citizenship can be revoked by the Central Government under Section 10 of the Citizenship Act, 1955. 
- Grounds include: 
  - Obtained by fraud. 
  - Disloyalty to the Constitution (act/speech against India). 
  - Imprisonment in India for 2+ years within the first 5 years of acquiring citizenship. 
  - Living abroad for 7+ years without declaring intent to retain Indian citizenship. 
- Proper safeguards exist to ensure due process.
	Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI)
	- Introduced as an alternative to dual citizenship (which India does not allow). 
- Merged with Person of Indian Origin (PIO) in 2015; now only OCI exists. 
- No need for a visa to travel to India; OCI card suffices. 
- No voting rights, government jobs, or agricultural land ownership. 
- Can open special NRI like bank accounts, make investments, adopt children in India. 
- Now requires renewal only once at 20 years of age.
	Non Resident Indian (NRI)
	- Indian passport holders residing abroad for work, business, or education. 
- Must stay outside India for 183+ days in a year to qualify as an NRI. 
- NRIs cannot buy agricultural or plantation land in India.
	Person of Indian Origin (PIO) 
	- Foreign citizens whose ancestors were Indian citizens/ permanent residents after the Constitution’s commencement. 
- PIO category was discontinued in 2015, m
erged into OCI.
	OCI vs NRI vs PIO
	- OCI: Lifelong visa-free travel to India; cannot vote, hold government jobs, or buy farm land. 
- NRI: Indian citizens abroad; can invest in India. Can vote but they just can’t vote remotely or by proxy unless specifically allowed. 
- PIO: No longer exists; merged with OCI in 2015.

Overview of FR
            * Fundamental means = imp all round dev (material, intellectual, moral, spiritual)
            * Written in constitution
            * No law against it by Parliament
            * Called "Magna carta" of india
            * Types
            * -ve (liberties)= limits authority of state- John Locke - "when there's no law , there is no freedom”. Cases reinforcing FRs-
            * Art 19- Shreya Singhal v. UOI (2015): Struck down Section 66A IT Act, reaffirming free speech as a supreme right.
            * Art 21- Maneka Gandhi v. UOI (1978): Expanded Article 21 to include the right to live with dignity. 
            * Art 25- Shayara Bano v. UOI (2017): Struck down Triple Talaq, reinforcing religious freedom within constitutional limits.
            * +ve (entitlement)- These rights require the state to actively promote welfare and equality. Cases interpreting FRs-
            * Art 21A- Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka (1992): Recognized education as a Fundamental Right.
            * Art 15(4) & 15(5)- Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992): Upheld OBC reservations in govt jobs to ensure social justice.
            * Art 39 A- Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979): Led to free legal aid for underprivileged prisoners.
            * Not absolute- reasonable restriction- Thomas Paine’s words ring true: "Eternal vigilance is the price of liberty.”

Article 20- Protection in respect of conviction of offences (only criminal cases)
                  * No ex - post facto law- No law shall declare any action from backdate as illegal. Article 20(1) does not apply to preventive detention, as it is not a punishment for a past act, but a measure for future prevention.
                  * No double jeopardy- Not punished more than once same offence (Not applicable in administrative or departmental authorities as they aren’t of judicial nature) 
                  * No self incrimination- No person shall be forced to give evidence against himself. 
                  * Doesn’t extend to compulsory production of material objects, give thumb impressions, signature & blood specimens & exhibition of body. 
                  * FYI- Audi Alteram Partem" is a principle of natural justice which means "hear the other side." before making a decision.


Article 21 - Protection of life & liberty
                  * No person shall be deprived of his life & liberty except according to procedure estb by law. It is available against arbitrary action of legislature & executive.
