Articles 22- Freedom against arrest and detention (only for criminal & quasi criminal cases) 
                  * Ordinary (Punitive) Detention: Article 22(1) & 22(2)
                  * The Concept: Detention takes place only after a formal trial and conviction in a court of law.
                  * Right to Grounds: The arrestee must be informed immediately of the reasons for their arrest.
                  * Right to Counsel: The arrestee has a fundamental right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of choice.
                  * Magistrate Production: The arrestee must be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours (excluding journey time).
                  * Release Rule: The arrestee must be released after 24 hours unless the magistrate authorises further detention.
                  * Preventive Detention: Article 22(3) to 22(7)
                  * The Concept: Detention is based on suspicion to prevent a future crime, skipping a trial and conviction.
                  * Right to Counsel Denied: Article 22(3)(b) explicitly denies the right to consult or be defended by a lawyer.
                  * Right to Grounds: The detenu must be informed of the grounds of detention.
                  * Public Interest Exception: The state can refuse to disclose facts if doing so goes against public interest.
                  * Right to Representation: The detenu must be given the earliest opportunity to make a representation against the detention order.
                  * Time Limit: Detention cannot exceed 3 months unless an Advisory Board (including High Court judges) approves an extension.
                  * The 44th Amendment Fact: The 44th Constitutional Amendment Act (1978) reduced this limit to 2 months, but it has not been brought into force.
                  * Lawmaking Powers
                  * Shared Authority: Both Parliament and State Legislatures (SL) can enact preventive detention laws.
                  * Exclusive Parliamentary Authority: Parliament has exclusive power for laws regarding Defence, Foreign Affairs, and Security of India.
                  * Concurrent Authority: Both Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws regarding the Security of a State, Public Order, and Maintenance of Essential Supplies & Services.
                  * Parliamentary Overrides: Parliament alone can prescribe the circumstances/classes of cases for detention beyond 3 months, specify the maximum period, and set Advisory Board procedures.
                  * Statistical Data: The NCRB ‘Crimes in India’ Report 2021 recorded over 1.1 lakh persons under preventive detention, the highest since 2017.
                  * Safeguards not applied to enemy alien, person detained under any law of preventive detention
                  * Cases
                  * AK Gopalan case 1950- SC upheld legality of preventive detention
                  * Ambedkar's justification - " In present circumstances, necessary for the executive against tampering with pub order, defense. This is exigency of liberty not abv pub interest. "

Criticism of Fundamental Rights
Criticism
	Details
	Excessive Limitations
	As Jaspat Roy Kapoor noted, FRs come with many restrictions.
	Lack of Socio-Economic Rights
	No rights like Right to Social Security, Right to Work (unlike USSR, China).
	Ambiguity & Legal Complexity
	Vague, indefinite, and called a "paradise of lawyers" (Sir Ivor Jennings).
	No Permanency
	Can be curtailed by the President (e.g., Right to Property).
	Political & Class Bias
	Benefits the wealthy (expensive remedies) and can be misused by ruling parties.
	No Consistent Philosophy
	Criticized by Sir Ivor Jennings for lacking a unified ideological base.
	Significance of Fundamental Rights
Significance
	Details
	Ensures Individual Freedom
	Golaknath case (1967): SC held that "FR enables man to chalk out life in the manner he likes best."
	Bedrock of Democracy
	Essential for democratic governance and rule of law.
	Protection & Safeguards
	Acts as a formidable bulwark of liberty and secures material, moral well-being.
	Protects Minority & Weak
	Strengthens secularism, shields vulnerable groups from oppression.
	Checks Absolute Power
	Prevents authoritarianism and ensures balance of power.
