Union Executive & State Executive: Election Mechanics, Impeachment, Ordinance Power, and Pardoning Powers

1. Introduction & Basics of the President


"We didn't give him any real power, but we have made his position one of authority and dignity. It wants to make neither a real executive nor a mere figurehead, but a head that neither reigns nor governs. It wants to make an excellent figurehead."
— Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru

  • Constitutional Head of India: The President of India is the ceremonial head of state. His constitutional position is broadly equivalent to the Monarch of the United Kingdom under a Parliamentary Cabinet system.
  • Part of the Union Executive: Mentioned under Part V (Articles 52 to 78) of the Constitution of India.
  • Core Articles (Articles 52–62): Covers the office, executive power, election, term of office, qualifications, conditions, oath, impeachment, and filling of vacancies of the President.
  • First Citizen of India: The President is designated as the first citizen of India and acts as the symbol of unity, integrity, and solidarity of the nation.


2. Election of the President (Articles 54–55)


Key Features of Presidential Election

  • Indirect Election: The President is elected not directly by the people, but by members of an Electoral College.
  • Electoral College Composition (Who Can Vote):
    • (a) The elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha).
    • (b) The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (MLAs).
    • (c) The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of Delhi, Puducherry (and Jammu & Kashmir).
  • Exclusions: Nominated members of Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and State Legislative Assemblies, as well as ALL members (elected or nominated) of State Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishad), do NOT participate in the election of the President.
  • Governing Legislation: The election is governed by the President and Vice-President Elections Act, 1952.
  • System of Election: Proportional Representation by means of the Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV) with secret balloting.
  • Winning Quota: To win the election, a candidate must secure a specific quota of votes, which is strictly more than 50% of the total value of valid votes polled.

Mathematical Formulas for Vote Values

1. Value of the Vote of an MLA:

$$\text{Value of MLA Vote} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Population of State}}{\text{Total Elected MLAs of State}} \right) \times \frac{1}{1000}$$


2. Value of the Vote of an MP:

$$\text{Value of MP Vote} = \frac{\text{Total Value of Votes of All MLAs of All States}}{\text{Total Number of Elected Members of Parliament (MPs)}}$$


Parity Principle: The total value of votes of all elected MPs across India is equal to the cumulative total value of votes of all elected MLAs across all States.

Impeachment Procedure of the President (Article 61)

Key Impeachment Principles:

  1. Ground for Impeachment: Strictly for "Violation of the Constitution" (not defined in the Constitution).
  2. Initiation: Motion can be initiated in either House of Parliament. Requires 14 days written notice signed by 1/4th of the total members of that House.
  3. Participation Distinction: Nominated members of both Houses participate in impeachment (though they cannot vote in the election). MLAs do NOT participate in impeachment.
  4. Majority Required: Must be passed by a Special Majority of 2/3rd of the Total Membership of each House.
  5. No Joint Sitting: There is no provision for a Joint Session in case of deadlock on an impeachment resolution.

4. Ordinance-Making Power (Article 123 vs. Article 213)


  • Article 123 (President) & Article 213 (Governor): Empowers the President and Governor to promulgate ordinances during the recess of Parliament or State Legislature.
  • Non-Discretionary Power: The President/Governor can issue an ordinance ONLY on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the PM / Chief Minister.
  • Not a Parallel Power of Legislation: Designed as an emergency measure to meet sudden circumstances, not to bypass Parliament or replace ordinary legislative procedure.
  • Retrospective Effect: An ordinance can be given retrospective operation.
  • Inapplicable to Constitutional Amendments: Ordinance power cannot be used to amend the Constitution.

Constitutional Safeguards & Timelines

  • Recess Condition: Promulgated ONLY when BOTH Houses of Parliament are not in session, OR when either of the two Houses is not in session (since a bill requires passage by both Houses).
  • 7th Schedule Scope: Co-extensive with the legislative power of Parliament (Union List, Concurrent List, and Residuary subjects).
  • 6-Week Reassembly Rule: Every ordinance issued during recess must be laid before both Houses of Parliament upon reassembly. It ceases to operate at the expiration of 6 weeks from the reassembly of Parliament (unless approved earlier).

Judicial Review & Amendments Timeline

  • 38th Constitutional Amendment Act (1975): Made the President's satisfaction regarding ordinance issuance final and non-justiciable (beyond Judicial Review).
  • 44th Constitutional Amendment Act (1978): Deleted the 38th Amendment clause. The satisfaction of the President/Governor is now open to Judicial Review if issued with mala fide intention.

5. Pardoning Powers (Article 72 vs. Article 161)


  • Article 72 (President): Empowers the President to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, or remissions of punishment, or to suspend, remit, or commute the sentence of any person convicted of an offense:
    • (1) In all cases where punishment is by a Court Martial (Military Court) (Exclusive to President).
    • (2) In all cases where punishment is for an offense against a Union Law.
    • (3) In all cases where the sentence is a Death Sentence (Capital Punishment) (Exclusive to President; Governor cannot pardon death sentence).
  • Article 161 (Governor): Covers offenses against State Laws. Governor cannot grant pardon for death sentences (can only suspend, remit, or commute).

The 5 Types of Pardoning Powers

  • 1. Full Pardon: Absolves the convict completely from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications (wipes out both conviction and sentence).
  • 2. Commutation: Substitution of one form of punishment for a lighter form (e.g., commuting a death sentence to life imprisonment).
  • 3. Remission: Reducing the quantum/duration of sentence without changing its character (e.g., reducing rigorous imprisonment from 7 years to 3 years).
  • 4. Respite: Awarding a lesser sentence in place of the one originally awarded due to special facts (e.g., pregnancy of a female offender or severe physical disability).
  • 5. Reprieve: Temporary stay of execution of a sentence (especially death sentence) for a temporary period to allow time to seek pardon.
