THE PRESIDENT, VICE-PRESIDENT & GOVERNOR

1. Union Executive Overview & President Basics


Components of the Union Executive (Part V, Articles 52 to 78):

  • 1. President of India (Head of State)
  • 2. Vice-President of India (Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha)
  • 3. Prime Minister of India (Head of Government)
  • 4. Council of Ministers (Aids and advises the President)
  • 5. Attorney General of India (Highest Law Officer)

"We didn't give him any real power, but we have made his position one of authority and dignity. It wants to make neither a real executive nor a mere figurehead, but a head that neither reigns nor governs. It wants to make an excellent figurehead."
— Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru

  • Constitutional Status: The President is the Head of the Indian State and the First Citizen of India. Represents the symbol of Unity, Integrity, and Solidarity of the nation.
  • Core Articles (Articles 52–62): Article 52 establishes that there shall be a President of India; Article 53 vests executive power in the President; Articles 54–55 govern elections.


2. Election of the President & Electoral Quota (Articles 54–55)


Electoral College Composition

  • Indirect Election: The President is elected by an Electoral College consisting of:
    • (a) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha).
    • (b) Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs).
    • (c) Elected members of UT Assemblies of Delhi & Puducherry (added by 70th CAA, 1992, effective 1995).
  • Excluded Members: Nominated members of Parliament, nominated MLAs of States, ALL members of Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishad), and nominated members of UT assemblies. (Note: 104th CAA, 2019 abolished Anglo-Indian nominations).
  • Assembly Dissolution Rule: If a state legislative assembly is dissolved, its members lose voting rights in the presidential election even if fresh elections have not been held.

Vote Value Formulas & Population Freeze

1. Value of the Vote of an MLA:

$$\text{Value of MLA Vote} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Population of State}}{\text{Total Elected MLAs of State}} \right) \times \frac{1}{1000}$$


2. Value of the Vote of an MP:

$$\text{Value of MP Vote} = \frac{\text{Total Value of Votes of All MLAs of All States}}{\text{Total Number of Elected MPs (LS + RS)}}$$


Population Freeze (84th CAA, 2001): For vote value calculations, 'Population' means the 1971 Census figures until the first census after 2026 is published.

Electoral Quota Formula: To win, a candidate must secure an absolute majority quota:

$$\text{Electoral Quota} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Valid Votes Polled}}{1 + 1} \right) + 1 = \left( \frac{\text{Total Valid Votes}}{2} \right) + 1 \quad (> 50\%)$$


Nomination, Deposit & Security Rules

  • Qualifications: Citizen of India, 35 years of age, qualified for election as a member of Lok Sabha, holds no office of profit under Union/State/Local bodies. (Sitting President, VP, Governors, Ministers are NOT office of profit).
  • Nomination Subscribers: Nomination must be proposed by at least 50 electors and seconded by 50 electors (increased in 1997 from 10/10 to curb non-serious candidates).
  • Security Deposit: ₹15,000 deposited in the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Forfeited if candidate fails to secure 1/6th of total valid votes polled.
  • Supreme Court Oversight: All election disputes are inquired into and decided exclusively by the Supreme Court whose decision is final. If an election is declared void, acts done by President before the declaration remain valid.


3. Vice-President of India (Article 66)


  • 2nd Highest Constitutional Office: Ranks 2nd in the official Warrant of Precedence (next to the President). Office modeled on the American Vice-President.
  • Electoral College Comparison:
    • Includes BOTH elected and nominated members of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha).
    • Does NOT include State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs).
    • Changed by 11th CAA, 1961 (removed requirement of a joint sitting of Parliament).
  • Qualifications: Citizen of India, 35 years of age, eligible for Rajya Sabha election (unlike President who requires Lok Sabha eligibility). Nomination requires 20 proposers + 20 seconders & ₹15,000 deposit.
  • Removal Procedure: No formal impeachment required. Removed by a resolution of Rajya Sabha passed by an Effective Majority (majority of all the then members) and agreed to by Lok Sabha by a Simple Majority. Requires 14 days advance notice.
  • Dual Functions:
    1. Ex-Officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha: Draws salary as RS Chairman (₹4 lakh/month).
    2. Acting President: Acts as President during vacancy (death, resignation, removal) for a maximum period of 6 months. Draws President's salary during this period.

Succession on Vacancy Acts as President temporarily for max 6 months until new election Succeeds to the Presidency and holds office for the remainder of unexpired term

Impeachment Procedure of the President (Article 61)

Quasi-Judicial Impeachment Process:

  1. Ground: Violation of the Constitution (Not defined in the Constitution).
  2. Initiation: Either House of Parliament. Requires 1/4th members signature & 14 days advance written notice to President.
  3. 1st House Voting: Passed by 2/3rd majority of Total Membership of the House.
  4. 2nd House Investigation: Second House investigates charges. President has right to appear and defend.
  5. 2nd House Voting: If 2nd House also passes by 2/3rd majority of Total Membership, President stands removed from date of resolution.
  6. Participation Distinction: Nominated MPs participate in impeachment (though excluded from election). Elected MLAs do NOT participate in impeachment. No President has ever been impeached.


5. 7 Categories of Powers & Emergency Powers


Overview of 7 Powers Categories

  • 1. Executive Powers: All actions taken in President's name; appoints PM, Ministers, AGI, CAG, CEC/ECs, UPSC, Governors, Finance Commission; Seeking info under Art 78.
  • 2. Legislative Powers: Summon/prorogue/dissolve Lok Sabha; Joint Sitting (Art 108); RS 12 nominations (Literature, Science, Art, Social Service); Laying CAG/UPSC/FC reports.
  • 3. Financial Powers: Money Bills prior recommendation; Budget laying; Contingency Fund advances; Finance Commission every 5 years.
  • 4. Judicial Powers: CJI & SC/HC Judges appointments; Advisory Opinion (Art 143); Pardoning Power (Art 72).
  • 5. Diplomatic Powers: International representation, treaties (subject to Parliamentary approval), sending/receiving ambassadors.
  • 6. Military Powers: Supreme Commander of Defense Forces; appoints Army/Navy/Air Force chiefs; declares war/peace (subject to Parliament approval).
  • 7. Emergency Powers: Extraordinary emergency powers under Articles 352, 356/365, and 360.

6. Veto Powers (Union vs. State Legislation)


Types of Vetoes Exercised by Indian President

  • Absolute Veto: Refusing assent altogether; bill ends. Used for Private Member Bills or Cabinet Bills after resignation. (Examples: PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954 - Dr. Rajendra Prasad; MP Salary Bill 1991 - R. Venkataraman).
  • Suspensive Veto: Returning a non-money bill for reconsideration. Overridden if Parliament re-passes by simple majority. (Cannot return Money Bills).
  • Pocket Veto: Taking no action on a bill indefinitely. (Example: Zail Singh 1986 Indian Post Office Amendment Bill).
  • Constitutional Amendment Bills: 24th CAA, 1971 made it mandatory for President to give assent to Constitutional Amendment Bills (No Veto applicable).

Presidential Veto Over State Legislation (Article 200 & Article 201)

7. Ordinance (Art 123) & Pardoning Principles (Art 72)


Ordinance Power Case Law Timeline (Art 123 vs Art 213)

  • Cooper Case (1970): Supreme Court ruled that President's satisfaction is justiciable on grounds of mala fide.
  • 38th Amendment Act (1975): Made satisfaction final and non-justiciable.
  • 44th Amendment Act (1978): Deleted 38th CAA clause; satisfaction restored to Judicial Review.
  • D.C. Wadhwa Case (1986): SC held that repromulgation of ordinances without placing before legislature is unconstitutional.
  • Lifespan Rule: Ordinance valid for maximum 6 months + 6 weeks (6 months max gap between sessions + 6 weeks after reassembly).

Types of Pardoning Powers (Article 72)

Article 72 empowers the President to grant pardons independent of the Judiciary (but on Cabinet advice). It applies to Union Law offenses, Court Martial sentences, and Death Sentences.

  • Full Pardon: Absolves the convict completely from all sentences, punishments, and disqualifications.
  • Commutation: Substitution of one form of punishment for a lighter form (e.g., death sentence to life imprisonment).
  • Remission: Reducing the quantum/duration of the sentence without changing its character (e.g., 7 years rigorous imprisonment to 3 years rigorous).
  • Respite: Awarding a lesser sentence in place of the original due to special facts (e.g., pregnancy of a female offender).
  • Reprieve: Temporary stay of execution of a sentence.

Kehar Singh Case (1988) Pardoning Power Principles (Art 72 vs Art 161)

Supreme Court Principles in Kehar Singh v. Union of India (1988):

  1. 1. Petitioner has no right to an oral hearing before the President.
  2. 2. President can re-examine evidence afresh and form a different opinion from the courts.
  3. 3. Power must be exercised on the advice of the Union Cabinet.
  4. 4. Supreme Court need not lay down specific guidelines for presidential clemency.
  5. 5. Limited Judicial Review: Judicial review is allowed ONLY if the decision is arbitrary, irrational, mala fide, or discriminatory.

8. Constitutional Immunity (Article 361) & Landmark Cases


Personal Immunity of President & Governor (Article 361):

  • Official Acts Immunity: Not answerable to any court for exercise and performance of powers and duties of office.
  • Criminal Proceedings Immunity: No criminal proceedings whatsoever shall be instituted or continued in any court during term of office. No process for arrest or imprisonment.
  • Civil Proceedings Requirement: Civil proceedings against personal acts require 2 months' prior written notice.
