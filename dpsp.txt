### **Introduction and Overview**
*   **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSPs)** by Prashant Tiwari.
*   **Enumeration:** Enumerated in **Part IV** of the Constitution from **Articles 36 to 51**.
*   **Origin:** Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution of 1937** (which drew from the Spanish Constitution).
*   **Key Descriptions:**
    *   **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Described them as **“novel features”** of the Constitution.
    *   **Granville Austin:** Called Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles together as the **“Conscience of the Constitution”**.
    *   **Soul of the Constitution:** Together they represent the philosophy and soul of the Constitution.

### **Features of Directive Principles**
*   **Meaning & Scope:**
    *   DPSPs are **ideals/goals** to guide the State in policy-making and law-making.
    *   They are constitutional instructions/recommendations to the legislature, executive, and administration.
    *   **Article 36:** "State" in Part IV has the same meaning as in Part III (Fundamental Rights), including Union and State governments, legislatures, executive bodies, local authorities, and all public authorities.
*   **Resemblance to Instrument of Instructions:**
    *   Similar to guidelines issued by the British Government to the Governor-General and Governors under the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
    *   Dr. Ambedkar viewed them as an **“Instrument of Instructions”** addressed to the legislature and executive instead of Governors.
*   **Programme for a Welfare State:**
    *   A comprehensive economic, social, and political programme for a modern democratic state.
    *   **Aim:** Realise the Preamble's ideals of justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity.
    *   **Objective:** Establish a **Welfare State**, not a "Police State" as under colonial rule.
    *   Promote economic and social democracy alongside political democracy.
*   **Non-Justiciable Nature (Article 37):**
    *   Not legally enforceable in courts; the government cannot be compelled to implement them.
    *   However, they are **fundamental in governance**, and the State is duty-bound to apply them when making laws.
*   **Aid to Judicial Interpretation:**
    *   DPSPs guide courts in interpreting laws.
    *   Supreme Court rulings state that if a law seeks to implement a DPSP, courts may uphold it as “reasonable” under **Article 14** (equality) or **Article 19** (freedoms) to defend its constitutionality.

### **Classification of Directive Principles**
The Constitution does not classify them, but they are usually divided into three categories based on content:
1.  **Socialistic Principles:** Reflect the ideology of democratic socialism to achieve social and economic justice.
    *   **Article 38:** Promotion of welfare by securing a social order permeated by justice (social, economic, political) and minimising inequalities.
    *   **Article 39:** Securing the right to livelihood, equitable distribution of resources, prevention of wealth concentration, equal pay for equal work, and protection of workers/children.
    *   **Article 39A:** Equal justice and free legal aid for the poor.
    *   **Article 41:** Right to work, education, and public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, or disability.
    *   **Article 42:** Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.
    *   **Article 43:** Living wage and decent standard of life for workers.
    *   **Article 43A:** Participation of workers in management of industries.
    *   **Article 47:** Raising nutrition levels and public health; prohibiting intoxicating drinks/drugs.
2.  **Gandhian Principles:** Based on Gandhi’s vision of rural upliftment and self-sufficient villages.
    *   **Article 40:** Organising village panchayats as units of self-government.
    *   **Article 43:** Promoting cottage industries in rural areas.
    *   **Article 43B:** Encouraging co-operative societies.
    *   **Article 46:** Promoting educational and economic interests of SCs, STs, and weaker sections.
    *   **Article 47:** Prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs.
    *   **Article 48:** Prohibiting the slaughter of cows, calves, and milch cattle.
3.  **Liberal-Intellectual Principles:** Focus on civil liberties, environment, and international peace.
    *   **Article 44:** Uniform Civil Code (UCC) for all citizens.
    *   **Article 45:** Early childhood care and education for children below 6 years.
    *   **Article 48:** Organising agriculture and animal husbandry on scientific lines.
    *   **Article 48A:** Environmental protection and safeguarding forests/wildlife.
    *   **Article 49:** Protection of monuments and heritage of national importance.
    *   **Article 50:** Separation of judiciary from executive in public services.
    *   **Article 51:** Promotion of international peace and security.

### **Amendments and New DPSPs**
*   **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Added four new DPSPs: **Article 39(f)** (child development), **Article 39A** (legal aid), **Article 43A** (worker participation), and **Article 48A** (environment).
*   **44th Amendment Act (1978):** Added **Article 38(2)**, directing the State to minimise inequalities in income, status, and opportunities.
*   **86th Amendment Act (2002):** Changed **Article 45** to provide early childhood care/education until age 6 (linked with Article 21A).
*   **97th Amendment Act (2011):** Added **Article 43B** regarding co-operative societies.

### **Sanction and Rationale**
*   **Constitutional Advice:** Sir B.N. Rau recommended dividing rights into **justiciable** (Fundamental Rights) and **non-justiciable** (Directive Principles).
*   **Practical Force:** While non-justiciable legally, they are **politically binding** through public opinion and elections.
*   **Reasons for Non-Justiciability:**
    *   Lack of financial resources at Independence.
    *   Practicality issues due to vast diversity and backwardness.
    *   Risk of overburdening the newly independent State's administrative capacity.
    *   A pragmatic view to leave implementation to progressive public opinion.

### **Criticisms of DPSPs**
*   **No Legal Force:** Described as “pious superfluities” (K.T. Shah), “New Year’s resolutions” (Nasiruddin Ahmad), a “veritable dustbin of sentiments” (T.T. Krishnamachari), and “pious aspirations” (Sir Ivor Jennings).
*   **Illogically Arranged:** Critics like N. Srinivasan argue they mix vital economic questions with unimportant issues.
*   **Conservative Nature:** Sir Ivor Jennings argued they reflected 19th-century English political philosophy (Fabian Socialism) and were outdated.
*   **Constitutional Conflict:** Potential for deadlocks between Centre-States (K. Santhanam), President-PM, or Governor-CM.

### **Conflict Between Fundamental Rights (FRs) and DPSPs**
*   **Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951):** FRs prevail over DPSPs; DPSPs are subsidiary to FRs.
*   **Golaknath Case (1967):** FRs are sacrosanct and cannot be abridged for DPSPs.
*   **25th Amendment Act (1971):** Inserted **Article 31C**, giving Article 39(b) and (c) primacy over Articles 14, 19, and 31.
*   **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973):** Established Basic Structure; upheld primacy of Article 39(b) and (c) over certain FRs.
*   **42nd Amendment (1976):** Attempted to give all DPSPs primacy over FRs.
*   **Minerva Mills Case (1980):** Struck down the extension of primacy; restored balance, noting FRs and DPSPs are "complementary like two wheels of a chariot".
*   **Present Position:** FRs generally enjoy supremacy, except for **Article 39(b) and (c)** overriding **Articles 14 and 19**.

### **Directives Outside Part IV**
*   **Article 335 (Part XVI):** Claims of SCs and STs to services.
*   **Article 350-A (Part XVII):** Instruction in mother tongue at the primary stage for linguistic minorities.
*   **Article 351 (Part XVII):** Development and promotion of the Hindi language.

### **I. Basics and Background**
*   **Enumeration:** Part IV, spanning from **Art. 36 - 51** of the Constitution.
*   **Origin:** Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution (1937)**.
*   **Recommendation:** Its non-justiciable nature was based on the recommendations of the **Tej Bahadur Sapru Committee Report (1944)**.
*   **Core Concepts:**
    *   **Positive Connotation:** Establishes social and economic democracy.
    *   **Welfare State:** Promotes the concept of a "Welfare State" and "Inclusive Growth".
    *   **Precursors:** Serves as the precursor to **Panchayati Raj Institutions** (Art. 40).
    *   **Fundamental in Governance:** According to **Article 37**, these principles are fundamental in the governance of the country and must be applied in making laws.

### **II. Key Perspectives and Definitions**
*   **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Described DPSPs as **"Novel features"** and the **"Soul of the Constitution"**; he also noted they aim for **"economic democracy"** as distinguished from political democracy.
*   **Granville Austin:** Called DPSP the **"Conscience of the Constitution"**.
*   **Instruments of Instruction:** Resembles the "Instruments of Instruction" found in the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
*   **B.N. Rau:** The Constitutional Advisor recommended the division of rights into **Justiciable (FR)** and **Non-justiciable (DPSP)**.

### **III. Nature and Purpose**
*   **Nature:** A blend of **Socialist, Gandhian, and Liberal principles**.
*   **Enforceability:** Non-justiciable, non-self-executory, and non-enforceable by a court of law; however, they are **fundamental guidelines** in governance.
*   **Goal:** To achieve the Preamble's ideals of social, economic, and political justice.
*   **Reasons for Non-Justiciability:**
    *   Administrative and financial limitations at the time of independence.
    *   Vast diversity, illiteracy, poverty, and backwardness of the country.
    *   Some principles have moral rather than legal connotations.

### **IV. Classification of Directive Principles**
While not formally classified in the Constitution, they are categorized based on their philosophy:

#### **1. Socialistic Principles**
Framework for a democratic socialist state to provide social and economic justice:
*   **Art. 38:** Secure a social order for people's welfare (e.g., Nationalisation of banks).
*   **Art. 39:** Principles of policy; 39(b) and (c) are exceptions to Art. 14 and 19.
*   **Art. 41:** Right to work, education, and public assistance (e.g., MGNREGA).
*   **Art. 42:** Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief (e.g., Maternity Benefit Act).
*   **Art. 43:** Living wage for workers (e.g., Minimum Wages Act).
*   **Art. 43A:** Participation of workers in management of industries.
*   **Art. 47 (Part 1):** Raise nutrition levels and public health (e.g., Mid-Day Meal).

#### **2. Gandhian Principles**
Based on Gandhi's programme of reconstruction:
*   **Art. 40:** Organisation of village panchayats.
*   **Art. 43 (Part 2):** Promote cottage industries.
*   **Art. 43B:** Promotion of co-operative societies (97th CAA 2011).
*   **Art. 46:** Promote interests of SCs, STs, and weaker sections.
*   **Art. 47 (Part 2):** Prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs.
*   **Art. 48 (Part 1):** Prohibit slaughter of cows and calves.

#### **3. Liberal-Intellectual Principles**
Ideology of liberalism:
*   **Art. 44:** Uniform Civil Code.
*   **Art. 45:** Childhood care and education for children below six.
*   **Art. 48 (Part 2):** Organize agriculture and animal husbandry on scientific lines.
*   **Art. 48A:** Protection of environment, forests, and wildlife.
*   **Art. 49:** Protection of monuments and places of national importance.
*   **Art. 50:** Separation of judiciary from executive.
*   **Art. 51:** Promotion of international peace and security.

**Legal Evolution of Conflict:**
*   **Champakam Dorairajan Case (1952):** FRs are superior; DPSPs are subsidiary.
*   **Kerala Education Bill (1957):** Propounded the **Doctrine of Harmonious Construction**.
*   **Golak Nath Case (1967):** FRs are sacrosanct and cannot be diluted.
*   **Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973):** Parliament can amend any part subject to the **Basic Structure**.
*   **Minerva Mills Case (1980):** Primacy of Art 39(b) and (c) over Art 14 and 19; established balance between FR and DPSP.
*   **Current Position:** FRs hold supremacy, but can be amended to implement DPSPs as long as the Basic Structure is not destroyed.

### **VI. Case Studies in Implementation**
#### **1. Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017**
*   **Bearing:** Implements **Art. 42**.
*   **Key Changes:** Paid maternity leave increased from **12 to 26 weeks** for the first two children.
*   **Provisions:** Includes 12 weeks for adoptive/commissioning mothers, mandatory crèche facilities for establishments with 50+ employees, and "work from home" options.

#### **2. MGNREGA**
*   **Bearing:** Implements **Art. 41**.
*   **Objective:** Guaranteed **100 days** of unskilled wage employment to rural households.
*   **Features:** Demand-driven approach, funded primarily by the Central Government, and managed by Gram Panchayats.

#### **3. Liquor Prohibition (Art. 47)**
*   **State Subject:** Entry 51 of the State List.
*   **Enforcement:** Complete prohibition in **Gujarat, Bihar, Nagaland, and Lakshadweep**.
*   **Highway Ban:** The Supreme Court banned liquor sales within **500 metres** of National/State highways to counter drunken driving.

#### **4. Uniform Civil Code (Art. 44)**
*   **Implementation:** **Uttarakhand** became the first state to implement it (Jan 2025), exempting Scheduled Tribes.
*   **Current Status:** Goa follows the **Portuguese Civil Code 1867**.
*   **Judicial Push:** Cases like **Shah Bano (1985)** and **Shayara Bano (2017)** (Triple Talaq) have highlighted the need for UCC.
*   **Conflict:** Pits Art. 25 & 26 (Religious freedom) against Art. 14 & 15 (Equality).

### **VII. Criticisms and Significance**
*   **Criticisms:** Described as a **"veritable dustbin of sentiments"** (T.T. Krishnamachari), a **"cheque on a bank payable only when resources permit"** (K.T. Shah), and **"Fabian Socialism without the socialism"** (Sir Ivor Jennings).
*   **Significance:**
    *   Serves as a **"Common Minimum Programme"** for any government.
    *   Acts as a **"Guiding Light"** for the judiciary to determine constitutional validity.
    *   Provides stability and continuity in policies despite changes in government.
    *   Serves as a benchmark for citizens to gauge government performance.

### **VIII. Directives Outside Part IV**
*   **Art. 335:** Claims of SCs and STs to services.
*   **Art. 350-A:** Instruction in mother tongue at the primary stage for linguistic minorities.
*   **Art. 351:** Development and promotion of the Hindi language.

▪In the aftermath of Independence, the Indian government did not impose a centralized regulation

▪Personal law subjects like marriage, divorce, inheritance come under Concurrent list.

CHALLENGES ASSOCIATED WITH UCC
1. Constitutional challenges
▪Freedom of religion gets into conflict with the right to equality.
o Art. 25 - lays down an individual’s fundamental right to religion.
o Art. 26(b) - upholds the right of each religious denomination or any section thereof to “manage its own
affairs in matters of religion”
o Art. 29 - defines the right to conserve distinctive culture.
o These rights gets into conflict with the equality before law enshrined under Art. 14 and 15.
▪Moreover, an individual’s freedom of religion under Art. 25 is subject to “public order, health,

▪In 2018, a report by the Law Commission of India stated that the UCC is “neither necessary nor

▪In the name of uniformity, the minorities fears that the culture of the majority is being imposed over

▪Patriarchal mindset of Indian society poses a big challenge in implementation of UCC.

• UCC will eliminate this overlapping of laws.
3. Simplification of Indian legal system:
• UCC will lead to reduction in litigation emanating from multiple personal laws.
4. Establishing a secular society:
• UCC will de-link law from religion which is a very desirable objective to achieve in a secular and

• Moreover, it fulfils constitutional mandates under Art. 44 of DPSP.
